Single-fold Diophantine representations (conceptual, result 242)

For a recursively enumerable set, a Diophantine polynomial has either no auxiliary witness or exactly one; the demo counts witnesses for concrete equations.

Context

Every recursively enumerable set of tuples of natural numbers has a Diophantine representation with exactly one auxiliary solution for each member and none for nonmembers. This proves the single-fold conjecture, hence the finite-fold conjecture: recursive enumerability is captured by polynomial equations over $\mathbb N$ with a unique witness. Diophantine solvability over the nonnegative integers remains undecidable even under an at-most-one-solution promise.

Relevance

This bridges computability and number theory: every r.e. condition becomes a polynomial equation, and the uniqueness of witnesses does not make the existence question decidable. The explorer below enumerates auxiliary variables in a bounded box, counts solutions for several concrete Diophantine equations, and shows when a representation is genuinely single-fold or has multiple witnesses.
