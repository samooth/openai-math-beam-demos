Maximal and variational bounds for the triangular Hilbert transform (conceptual, result 082)

The triangular Hilbert transform is the bilinear singular integral with kernel \(1/t\) coupling \(F(x+t,y)\) and \(G(x,y+t)\). Result 082 proves maximal and annular \(r\)-variation bounds, \(r>2), from complex (L^3(\mathbb R^2)\times L^3(\mathbb R^2)) to (L^{3/2}(\mathbb R^2)), controlling both hard-truncation endpoints and giving a.e. and norm convergence; pairing with a third input settles the symmetric (L^3\times L^3\times L^3) point. Tune the inner/outer truncations, the variation exponent, and the base point.

Context

Proves maximal and annular \(r\)-variation bounds, for every \(r>2), from complex (L^3(\mathbb R^2)\times L^3(\mathbb R^2)) to (L^{3/2}(\mathbb R^2)). The maximal estimate controls both hard-truncation endpoints and gives almost-everywhere and norm convergence of the truncated triangular Hilbert integrals. On a common full-measure set the truncated integrals are defined, and the maximal output is almost-everywhere measurable. Pairing with a third input settles the triangular Hilbert transform estimate at the symmetric (L^3\times L^3\times L^3) point.

Relevance

Supplies the missing maximal and variational control for the triangular Hilbert transform at the symmetric exponent, enabling almost-everywhere and norm convergence and the trilinear \(L^3\) endpoint. The inner and outer truncation parameters and the \(r>2) variation quantify robustness of the singular integral, while the exponent pair (L^3\times L^3\to L^{3/2}) matches scaling and feeds multilinear harmonic analysis. The third-input pairing promotes the bilinear bound to the full trilinear triangular Hilbert transform estimate.
