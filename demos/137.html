One-tape time simulation in two-fifths-power space (conceptual, result 137)

Determines the halting and finite-control outcome of a fixed deterministic one-writable-tape machine up to time (T) using (O(T^{2/5}\log^C(T+2))) space, improving the square-root exponent.

Context

Given a fixed deterministic machine with one writable tape, finitely many read-only input heads, and initial contents independent of (T), decide whether it halts within (T) steps and, if so, its finite control state. A machine head moves at most one cell per step, and tape contents and input symbols have polylogarithmic-space access. Result 137 performs this decision in (O(T^{2/5}\log^C(T+2))) space, for some constant (C), with unrestricted simulation time. This improves the classical Hopcroft--Paul--Valiant square-root space bound (O(T^{1/2})) for one-tape time simulation. The exponent (2/5) arises from a sharper space-time tradeoff in checkpointing and recursive middle-configuration search on a single tape.

Relevance

One-tape time simulation is a central space-time tradeoff problem: a naive step-by-step simulation uses (O(T)) space, while sublinear methods exploit the locality of a single tape head. Lowering the exponent from (1/2) to (2/5) narrows the gap between known upper and lower bounds and refines our understanding of how much memory is necessary to predict the future of a simple sequential machine. The interactive view compares the naive linear bound, the previous square-root bound, and the new two-fifths bound as functions of the time horizon (T), and reports the resulting space savings.
