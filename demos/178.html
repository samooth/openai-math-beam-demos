Deterministic nonbipartite Ramanujan graphs in every fixed degree (conceptual, result 178)

For fixed degree (d\ge 3), a (d)-regular graph is Ramanujan when every nontrivial adjacency eigenvalue lies in ((-2\sqrt{d-1},2\sqrt{d-1})). Adjust (d), (n), and the randomization level, then regenerate to compare a deterministic base graph with randomized regular graphs and inspect their spectra.

Context

Result 178 proves that for every fixed (d\ge 3) there is a simple (d)-regular nonbipartite Ramanujan graph on every sufficiently large even number (n) of vertices: every nonconstant adjacency eigenvalue is strictly between (-2\sqrt{d-1}) and (2\sqrt{d-1}). Moreover a deterministic algorithm outputs the full adjacency list in polynomial bit time, with an exponent that may depend on (d). Thus explicit optimal expanders exist in every fixed degree, without relying on random graphs or unproved number-theoretic constructions.

Relevance

Ramanujan graphs are optimal spectral expanders: their second eigenvalue is as small as possible, giving near-optimal mixing, hashing, error-correcting codes, and network routing. Making the construction deterministic and nonbipartite removes bipartition constraints and randomness, so these expanders can be generated reliably at any large even size.
