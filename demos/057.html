Campana’s abelianity conjecture and special varieties (conceptual, result 057)

Special compact Kähler manifolds have virtually abelian fundamental groups; special quasi-projective varieties have two-step nilpotent monodromy. Choose a geometry and see how Campana-specialness forces abelianity or nilpotency.

Context

Proves Campana's abelianity conjecture: special compact Kähler manifolds have virtually abelian fundamental groups. Using this theorem, establishes the same conclusion for order-two root orbifolds of smooth projective complex fourfolds along one nonempty smooth connected divisor, when special in the stated differential-line sense. For smooth special complex quasi-projective varieties, proves that every finite-dimensional complex linear representation of the fundamental group has virtually nilpotent image of class at most two.

Relevance

Links Campana's notion of specialness (no general-type quotient) to the algebraic structure of (\pi_1): special manifolds cannot carry complicated fundamental groups, and their finite-dimensional monodromy is at most 2-step nilpotent. This constrains non-abelian Hodge, moduli of varieties, and orbifold fundamental groups, and separates special varieties from general-type or hyperbolic ones.
