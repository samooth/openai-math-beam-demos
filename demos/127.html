The asymptotic Gotsman--Linial conjecture (conceptual, result 127)

Proves that a degree-at-most-(d) polynomial threshold function on the uniform (n)-dimensional Boolean cube has average sensitivity at most (8d\sqrt n), uniformly for (1\le d\le n). Average sensitivity counts expected output changes under single-bit flips.

Context

A polynomial threshold function (PTF) is (f(x)=\operatorname{sign}(p(x))) with (p) a real polynomial of degree at most (d) on the Boolean cube (\{\pm 1\}^n). Its average sensitivity is (\mathbb{E}_x[#\{i:f(x)\neq f(x\oplus e_i)\}]), the expected number of coordinates whose flip changes the output. Result 127 establishes the asymptotic Gotsman--Linial conjecture: for every such PTF, this quantity is at most (8d\sqrt n), uniformly for (1\le d\le n), allowing (\operatorname{sign}(0)=1). This replaces the trivial bound (n) with a bound growing only like (d\sqrt n).

Relevance

Average sensitivity governs noise stability, learning complexity, and the influence of variables in Boolean analysis. The (8d\sqrt n) bound shows that low-degree threshold functions are far more stable than worst-case Boolean functions, and it is tight up to constants for the asymptotic regime. The interactive view compares the bound with the trivial (n) bound and samples random PTFs on small cubes to verify that their measured average sensitivity stays below (8d\sqrt n).
