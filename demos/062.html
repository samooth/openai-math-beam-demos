The LeBrun--Salamon conjecture and projective contact classification (conceptual, result 062)

A closed connected positive quaternionic-Kähler manifold (M^{4m}) ((m\ge2)) has parallel curvature and is homothetic to a compact symmetric Wolf space. Its twistor space is a contact Fano manifold; tune (m) and (b_2) to see whether the contact manifold is an adjoint variety or a projectivized cotangent bundle.

Context

Proves the LeBrun--Salamon conjecture: every closed connected positive quaternionic-Kähler manifold of real dimension at least eight is homothetic to a compact symmetric Wolf space, so its Riemann curvature is parallel. Through the twistor construction, the associated contact Fano manifold is classified: if (b_2=1) it is an adjoint variety (Y_{\mathfrak g}) with its canonical contact structure, and if (b_2\ge2) its underlying manifold is (\mathbb P(T^*Z)) for a smooth projective variety (Z). The contact-Fano homogeneity conjecture is also resolved positively.

Relevance

Turns a positivity hypothesis on curvature into a complete symmetry classification: positive quaternionic-Kähler manifolds are not exotic but compact symmetric Wolf spaces. This underwrites twistor theory, contact-Fano homogeneity, and Kähler--Einstein metrics on contact manifolds, with consequences for rigidity, moduli, and homogeneous geometry in real dimensions (4m).

Reading the picture

The left oval is (M^{4m}) with positive scalar curvature; its vertical fibers are the twistor (S^2)-bundle. The right side is the twistor space (X), a contact Fano manifold of complex dimension (2n+1). When (b_2=1), (X) is a homogeneous adjoint variety (Y_{\mathfrak g}); when (b_2\ge2), (X) is a projectivized cotangent bundle (\mathbb P(T^*Z)). In both cases the LeBrun--Salamon theorem forces (M) to be a Wolf space.
