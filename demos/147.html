The near-boundary Birkhoff conjecture (conceptual, result 147)

A smooth strictly convex planar billiard is an ellipse whenever a full grazing annulus near its boundary is continuously foliated by invariant curves of the billiard map, equivalently by a continuous collar of smooth convex caustics.

Context

A planar billiard is strictly convex, smooth, and of positive curvature. Result 147 proves near-boundary rigidity: if a full grazing annulus (a thin region adjacent to the boundary) is continuously foliated by individually invariant essential curves of the billiard map, then the boundary is an ellipse. Equivalently, a continuous physical collar of smooth closed convex caustics near the boundary suffices. Thus local integrable structure at the boundary propagates to the global elliptical shape, resolving the near-boundary case of the Birkhoff conjecture.

Relevance

This identifies ellipses as the only convex billiards admitting a continuous caustic collar near the boundary, sharpening the link between caustic families, integrability, and spectral/length rigidity. It informs inverse billiard problems, stability of nearly elliptical tables, and the classification of integrable convex billiards.
