Deterministic polynomial factorization over prime fields (conceptual, result 142)

Gives a uniform deterministic algorithm that completely factors every nonzero dense degree-$n$ polynomial over a prime field $\mathbb F_p$, including multiplicities, in bit complexity polynomial in $(n+1)\log p$. The prime is supplied in binary. No randomness, integer-factorization or primitive-root oracle, or GRH assumption is required.

Context

Let $f\in\mathbb F_p[x]$ be nonzero of degree $n$, with $p$ prime. Result 142 gives a deterministic algorithm that outputs all irreducible factors of $f$ with their multiplicities, using $\operatorname{poly}((n+1)\log p)$ bit operations. The prime $p$ is given in binary; no randomness, integer factorization or primitive-root oracle, and no GRH is used. The proof uses the uniform Hecke zero-free theorem from the companion paper on primitive roots for admissible bases. Unlike randomized Cantor-Zassenhaus or GRH-dependent deterministic methods, the runtime bound is unconditional and uniform over all inputs and all primes.

Relevance

This removes randomness and number-theoretic oracles from polynomial factorization over finite fields, a basic subroutine in computer algebra, coding theory, and cryptography. It makes factorization of dense polynomials over prime fields a deterministic polynomial-time problem in the bit size of the input, clarifying the complexity of arithmetic in $\mathbb F_p[x]$ and enabling reproducible factorization pipelines without heuristic assumptions.
