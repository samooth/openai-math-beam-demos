Generalized star height at most three (conceptual, result 134)

Every regular language over a finite alphabet has a generalized regular expression with at most three nested Kleene stars, allowing union, concatenation and complement over the same alphabet. This establishes an absolute bound independent of automaton size, resolving the uniform-boundedness version of the generalized star-height problem.

Context

Generalized regular expressions permit union, concatenation, Kleene star and complement. Result 134 shows that every regular language over a finite alphabet can be written with at most three nested Kleene stars, so the generalized star height is uniformly bounded by $3$. The bound does not depend on the number of states of a recognizing automaton, settling the uniform-boundedness version of the generalized star-height problem.

Relevance

Classical star height can grow with the size of a minimal automaton, while allowing complement collapses the nesting depth to a small constant. This affects regular-language descriptions, automaton-to-expression conversion, and the relation between star depth, complementation and finite-monoid complexity.
