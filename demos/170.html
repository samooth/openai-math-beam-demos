Sharp logarithmic exponents for off-diagonal Ramsey numbers (conceptual, result 170)

For fixed (s\ge 5), the off-diagonal Ramsey number satisfies (r(s,t)=t^{s-1}/(\log t)^{s-2+o(1)}). Tune (s), (t), the (o(1)) term, and a constant to see how the logarithmic factor changes the growth.

Context

For every fixed integer \(s\ge 5\), result 170 proves \(r(s,t)=t^{s-1}/(\log t)^{s-2+o(1)}\) as \(t\to\infty\), determining the logarithmic exponent and matching the classical upper bound at that scale. Here \(r(s,t)\) is the least number of vertices forcing an \(s\)-clique or a \(t\)-vertex independent set. For \(s=5\) this is \(t^4/(\log t)^{3+o(1)}\); for each \(\varepsilon>0) the lower bound (t^{s-1}/(\log t)^{s-2+\varepsilon}) holds eventually, while the upper bound is (C_st^{s-1}/(\log t)^{s-2}).

Relevance

Off-diagonal Ramsey numbers measure the unavoidable transition from cliques to independent sets; pinning the logarithmic exponent closes the gap between the classical upper bound and the true asymptotic order. It clarifies how slowly the logarithmic correction decays, guides extremal constructions, and feeds the broader program of Ramsey growth rates and probabilistic method thresholds.
