Uniform identity testing for noncommutative formulas (conceptual, result 116)

For each characteristic, deterministic polynomial-time construction of a polynomial-dimensional matrix tuple that detects every nonzero division-free noncommutative formula of bounded size; rational formulas over $\mathbb Q$ admit polynomial-size hitting lists when a rational-matrix evaluation is defined.

Context

For each characteristic, constructs in deterministic polynomial bit time a polynomial-dimensional matrix tuple detecting every nonzero division-free noncommutative formula of bounded size over any field of that characteristic. Rational formulas over $\mathbb Q$ also admit polynomial-size hitting lists whenever they have a defined rational-matrix evaluation. The construction is uniform: one matrix tuple works for all formulas up to the given size bound, over every field of the chosen characteristic, removing dependence on the formula instance.

Relevance

This gives a uniform, deterministic polynomial-time identity test for noncommutative formulas, a setting where commutativity cannot be assumed and classical scalar Schwartz-Zippel tests fail. It separates nonzero formulas from the zero polynomial without random choices and with polynomially many matrix entries, with extensions to rational formulas via finite hitting lists. It underpins derandomization of algebraic complexity and efficient verification of noncommutative computations.
