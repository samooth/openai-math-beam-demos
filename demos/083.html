Stein's conjecture for Hilbert transforms along Lipschitz directions (conceptual, result 083)

Proves a uniform strong (L^2) bound for the planar Hilbert transform along any Lipschitz unit vector field, at integration lengths bounded by an absolute multiple of its reciprocal Lipschitz constant. The estimate is uniform over inner truncations and yields an (L^2)-bounded principal-value operator, establishing Stein's weak-type conjecture at this short scale.

Context

Proves a uniform strong (L^2) bound for the planar Hilbert transform along any Lipschitz unit vector field, at integration lengths bounded by an absolute multiple of its reciprocal Lipschitz constant. The estimate is uniform over inner truncations and yields an (L^2)-bounded principal-value operator, establishing Stein's weak-type conjecture at this short scale. The integration length is comparable to (1/K) where (K) is the Lipschitz constant, and the inner truncation may be sent to zero.

Relevance

Gives a concrete short-scale regime in which Hilbert transforms along non-smooth (Lipschitz) directions are uniformly (L^2)-bounded. The threshold (L\sim 1/K) separates lengths where the Lipschitz geometry is controlled from longer scales where additional curvature or averaging arguments are needed. It supports harmonic analysis of singular integrals on variable (Lipschitz) directions and the broader program around Stein's conjecture.
