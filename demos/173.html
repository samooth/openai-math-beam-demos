Seymour's second-neighborhood conjecture (conceptual, result 173)

Explores oriented graphs and verifies, vertex by vertex, that some vertex has at least as many second out-neighbors as first out-neighbors.

Context

An oriented graph has no loops and at most one directed edge between any two vertices. For a vertex $v$, let $N^+(v)$ be its out-neighbors and $N^{++}(v)$ the vertices at directed distance exactly two from $v$, excluding $v$ and $N^+(v)$. Seymour's second-neighborhood conjecture asserts that every nonempty finite oriented graph contains a vertex $v$ with $|N^{++}(v)| \ge |N^+(v)|$. Result 173 proves this assertion.

Relevance

This gives a universal local property of directed graphs: no oriented graph can have every vertex with strictly more first out-neighbors than second out-neighbors. It resolves a long-standing extremal conjecture and supports structural methods for oriented graphs, including minimum-degree and score-sequence arguments.
