The David-Semmes Riesz-transform problem in higher codimension (conceptual, result 081)

Resolves the remaining higher-codimension Riesz-transform rectifiability problem: for (d\ge4) and (2\le n\le d-2), an (n)-Ahlfors-David regular Radon measure on (\mathbb R^d) is uniformly (n)-rectifiable whenever its (n)-dimensional Riesz transform is uniformly (L^2)-bounded over all positive hard truncations. The rectifiability bounds depend only on dimension, regularity and operator bounds.

Context

Resolves the remaining higher-codimension Riesz-transform rectifiability problem: for (d\ge4) and (2\le n\le d-2), an (n)-Ahlfors-David regular Radon measure on (\mathbb R^d) is uniformly (n)-rectifiable whenever its (n)-dimensional Riesz transform is uniformly (L^2)-bounded over all positive hard truncations. The rectifiability bounds depend only on dimension, regularity and operator bounds. Hard truncations avoid the nonintegrable singularity at the origin; uniformity over all truncation scales is the operative hypothesis.

Relevance

Closes the higher-codimension case of the David-Semmes program: boundedness of a singular integral operator (the (n)-dimensional Riesz transform) characterizes uniform rectifiability of the underlying measure. It extends rectifiability criteria beyond codimension-one settings and supplies quantitative bounds depending only on ambient dimension, measure dimension, Ahlfors regularity and operator bounds. This underpins geometric measure theory, singular integrals and analytic capacity arguments in higher codimension.
