The circulant Hadamard conjecture (conceptual, result 179)

Explores the classification (n\in\{1,4\}) for real circulant Hadamard matrices and the resulting Barker-sequence lengths (\{2,3,4,5,7,11,13\}) by brute-force search over sign vectors.

Context

A real Hadamard matrix of order (n) has entries (\pm1) and satisfies (HH^{\mathsf T}=nI_n). It is circulant when every row is a cyclic shift of the first row, encoded by signs (h_0,\dots,h_{n-1}). The Hadamard condition is equivalent to vanishing periodic autocorrelations (P_h(t)=\sum_j h_jh_{j+t\bmod n}=0) for (1\le t1) exactly in (\{2,3,4,5,7,11,13\}).

Relevance

This settles the circulant Hadamard conjecture and completes the elementary classification of Barker sequences. It connects exact sign-pattern orthogonality to cyclic difference sets and cyclotomic arithmetic, and reduces the remaining candidate orders to none. The demo illustrates the vanishing-autocorrelation criterion directly: it searches all sign vectors for small orders, displays a witness when one exists, and shows the minimal possible autocorrelation obstruction otherwise. For Barker sequences it performs the same exhaustive check and recovers exactly the seven admissible lengths.
