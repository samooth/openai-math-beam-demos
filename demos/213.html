No infinite critical clusters on quasi-transitive graphs (conceptual, result 213)

Simulates Bernoulli bond percolation on a finite square lattice. Edges are open independently with probability $p$; vertices connected by open edges form clusters. The exact critical value is $p_c=1/2$.

Context

Bernoulli bond percolation on an infinite connected locally finite quasi-transitive graph opens each edge independently with probability $p$. Let $p_c$ be the critical probability for the existence of an infinite open cluster. Result 213 resolves the Benjamini--Schramm criticality conjecture: if $p_c<1$, then at $p=p_c$ there is almost surely no infinite open cluster. The family also proves the same conclusion for both nearest-neighbor bond and site percolation on $\mathbb Z^3$. The finite square lattice used here has exact critical value $p_c=1/2$ for bond percolation.

Relevance

This makes the phase transition sharp: below $p_c$ connection probabilities decay exponentially and all clusters are finite; at $p_c$ there is still no infinite cluster; above $p_c$ an infinite cluster appears (and is unique on amenable graphs such as $\mathbb Z^d$). The absence of critical infinite clusters underpins critical exponents, scaling limits, and universality in percolation. The interactive view shows a finite box: at $p=p_c$ the largest cluster remains sublinear, with many medium clusters and occasional spanning clusters, while $p>p_c$ produces a giant component.
