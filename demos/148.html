The dimension formula for self-similar measures on the line (conceptual, result 148)

For a self-similar measure on (\mathbb R) generated by finitely many contracting similarities with probabilities (p_i), the Hausdorff dimension is (\dim_{\mathrm H}\mu=\min\{1,h_{\mathrm{RW}}/\chi\}), where (h_{\mathrm{RW}}) is the entropy rate of the random compositions and (\chi) the Lyapunov exponent. This holds without separation, allowing exact overlaps and unequal contractions.

Context

A self-similar measure is the stationary distribution of an iterated function system (f_i(x)=r_i x+t_i) chosen with probabilities (p_i), with (0<|r_i|<1\). Its entropy rate is \(h_{\mathrm{RW}}=-\sum_i p_i\log p_i\) and its Lyapunov exponent is \(\chi=-\sum_i p_i\log|r_i|\). Result 148 proves \(\dim_{\mathrm H}\mu=\min\{1,h_{\mathrm{RW}}/\chi\}\) for every such measure on the line, resolving the entropy-rate dimension conjecture with no separation condition: exact overlaps and unequal contraction ratios are allowed. The companion homogeneous and attractor consequences give the usual entropy-over-Lyapunov formula and \(\dim_{\mathrm H}K=\min\{1,s\}\) where \(\sum_i|r_i|^s=1\). Absolute continuity is outside these statements.

Relevance

This removes the open-set or strong-separation hypothesis from the central dimension formula for self-similar measures, so overlapping constructions such as Bernoulli convolutions and unequal-ratio systems are covered directly. It quantifies how much dimension is lost to entropy per unit contraction and caps at the ambient dimension (1), informing multifractal analysis, fractal geometry, and the study of self-similar measures with overlaps.
