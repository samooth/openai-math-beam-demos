Exponential semidefinite complexity of perfect matching (conceptual, result 126)

Every exact semidefinite lift of the perfect matching polytope has exponential size. Even the positive semidefinite rank of its odd-cut slack matrix, after any fixed shift (0<\rho<1\), is \(2^{\Omega(n)}\), allowing arbitrary real positive semidefinite factors.

Context

The perfect matching polytope of (K_n) is described by nonnegativity and odd-cut constraints. A semidefinite lift represents it as a projection of an affine slice of a positive semidefinite cone. Result 126 answers Rothvoss's polynomial-size lift question negatively: for every fixed (0<\rho<1\), the matrix indexed by odd vertex sets \(U\) and perfect matchings \(M\) of \(K_n\), with entries \(|M\cap \delta(U)|-1+\rho\), has real positive semidefinite rank \(2^{\Omega(n)}\) as even \(n\) grows. Consequently every exact semidefinite lift of the perfect matching polytope has exponential size.

Relevance

This rules out polynomial-size semidefinite programming relaxations for exact perfect matching and shows that extended formulations using positive semidefinite cones cannot beat exponential size for this polytope. It separates semidefinite lifts from linear lifts in complexity and motivates combinatorial or randomized approaches for large matching instances.
