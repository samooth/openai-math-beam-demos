No bigeodesics and smooth limit shapes in planar first-passage percolation (conceptual, result 212)

Samples iid edge weights on a finite square lattice, computes first-passage times from the origin by Dijkstra, and traces a geodesic to a boundary point. Isochrones approximate the limit shape; varying the Gamma law illustrates its smoothness and anisotropy.

Context

Planar first-passage percolation assigns iid nonnegative weights to the edges of $\mathbb Z^2$; the passage time between two points is the minimum total weight along a lattice path. The theorem proves that, for nonatomic weights whose four-edge minimum has finite second moment, there is no doubly infinite geodesic. For exponential weights the limit shape is strictly convex with $C^1$ boundary; for every Gamma law with positive shape and rate, the time-constant norm is differentiable away from the origin.

Relevance

Excludes bigeodesics in the planar case, supporting uniqueness of geodesics and the absence of doubly infinite optimal paths. Differentiability and strict convexity of the limit shape yield a well-defined normal and supporting line at every boundary point, which underpins fluctuation theory, geodesic uniqueness, and universality in random growth models. The interactive view resamples a finite grid, computes passage times, and traces one geodesic to visualize anisotropic growth and approximate isochrones for different Gamma parameters.
