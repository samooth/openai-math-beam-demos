Directional zero–one laws and ballisticity in random environments (conceptual, result 220)

For an RWRE on $\mathbb Z^d$, directional escape probabilities are forced to $0$ or $1$ under strict or uniform ellipticity, and almost-sure directional transience gives a deterministic ballistic velocity whose positive-projection hemisphere is exactly the set of transient directions.

Context

Studies random walks in random environments on $\mathbb Z^d$. For iid strictly elliptic nearest-neighbor environments with $d\ge3$, and for stationary ergodic finite-range-dependent environments under uniform ellipticity, the probability of escape in any fixed nonzero direction is either $0$ or $1$ under the annealed law from the origin. For iid uniformly elliptic environments with $d\ge2$, almost-sure directional transience implies ballisticity: $X_n/n$ converges to a deterministic velocity with positive projection in that direction. For $d\ge3$, positive probability of transience in a direction already suffices; the velocity is unique and the transient directions are exactly the open hemisphere of directions with positive inner product with it.

Relevance

Turns a qualitative directional question into a deterministic dichotomy and a velocity. It rules out nontrivial intermediate escape probabilities, gives a sharp geometric criterion for transience as an open hemisphere, and connects local ellipticity assumptions to global ballistic transport. The interactive view varies the velocity and dimension to show which directions are transient and how the hemisphere rotates.
