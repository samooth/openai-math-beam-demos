Sharp three- and four-state reconstruction thresholds (conceptual, result 229)

Conceptual view of broadcasting on a tree: a root spin in $q$ states is propagated with channel parameter $\lambda$, and one asks whether the root can be estimated from the leaves. The sharp threshold is $d\lambda^2>1$.

Context

Studies exact reconstruction thresholds for symmetric three-state and ferromagnetic four-state broadcasting on regular trees ($d\ge2$) and observed Poisson trees (mean $d>1$ and $d>0$, respectively). Reconstruction holds when $d\lambda^2>1$, with nonreconstruction at equality, and the Poisson advantage is averaged over trees and root spins without conditioning on survival. The three-state theorem allows both signs of $\lambda$ and gives the exact weak-recovery threshold for the symmetric three-community stochastic block model.

Relevance

Identifies the sharp Kesten--Stigum-type phase transition for multi-state channels: above threshold the root leaves a nonvanishing imprint on the leaves, while at or below threshold all information is lost in the limit. This yields the weak-recovery threshold for the symmetric three-community stochastic block model.
