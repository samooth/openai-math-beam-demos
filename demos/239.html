Sharp singularity rates for symmetric random sign matrices (conceptual, result 239)

For an \(n\times n\) symmetric matrix whose entries on and above the diagonal are independent signs, the singularity probability is sharp: uniform signs give \(\Pr(\det A_n=0)=(1/2+o(1))^n\), and for bias \(p\neq 1/2\) the rate is \((p^2+(1-p)^2+o(1))^n\), attained by two agreeing rows.

Context

Determines the sharp exponential singularity rate of symmetric random sign matrices with independent entries on and above the diagonal. Uniform signs give \(\Pr(\det A_n=0)=(1/2+o(1))^n\); for fixed bias \(p\in(0,1)\setminus\{1/2\}\), the rate is \((p^2+(1-p)^2+o(1))^n\). In the biased case, agreeing rows attain this rate.

Relevance

Quantifies how often a random symmetric sign matrix is singular, separates the uniform and biased regimes, and identifies the extremal mechanism: in the biased case two identical rows attain the sharp rate. This sharpens random-matrix singularity theory and informs combinatorial probability, random graphs, and arithmetic applications where sign matrices appear.
