Sinai's positive-entropy conjecture for the standard map (conceptual, result 146)

The standard map on the two-dimensional torus is an area-preserving map; slide the parameter $K$ and watch regular islands give way to global chaos, with a positive finite-time Lyapunov exponent signaling positive metric entropy.

Context

The standard map (Chirikov map) $(q,p)\mapsto (q+p+K\sin q,;p+K\sin q)$ on $\mathbb T^2$ is area-preserving. Sinai's conjecture asserts positive metric entropy with respect to area for every sufficiently large (indeed all positive) parameter $K$. Result 146 proves positive metric entropy for every sufficiently large positive $K$, with a full tail of parameters, establishing the conjecture for the original family. In the chaotic regime, nearby orbits separate exponentially and information grows linearly under iteration.

Relevance

Positive metric entropy is the measure-theoretic signature of chaos: it underpins transport, diffusion, and statistical properties of Hamiltonian systems and connects to ergodic theory, dynamical zeta functions, and the foundations of statistical mechanics. The full parameter tail removes exceptional sets, giving uniform conclusions for large coupling.
