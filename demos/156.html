Borsuk's conjecture fails in dimension nine (conceptual, result 156)

A compact set of rank-one orthogonal projectors onto lines in $\mathbb R^4$, measured by the Frobenius metric, lies in a nine-dimensional affine space and has diameter $\sqrt2$. The demo samples points of this projective set, connects diameter pairs (orthogonal lines), and greedily partitions them into $k$ color classes.

Context

Borsuk's conjecture predicts that every bounded subset of $\mathbb R^d$ can be covered by $d+1$ sets of strictly smaller diameter. Result 156 constructs a compact subset of $\mathbb R^9$ that cannot be covered by ten such sets. The example is the set of rank-one orthogonal projectors onto lines in $\mathbb R^4$, equipped with the Frobenius metric; it has diameter $\sqrt2$.

Relevance

This lowers the known dimension in which Borsuk's conjecture fails to nine, sharpening the combinatorial/topological obstruction and guiding the search for the smallest counterexample dimension. It highlights the role of projective geometry and orthogonal incidence in high-dimensional covering problems.
