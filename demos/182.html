Power savings for polynomial-difference-free sets (conceptual, result 182)

For an intersective polynomial $h$ of degree $k\ge2$, a subset of $\{1,\dots,N\}$ may not contain two elements whose difference is a nonzero value $h(t)$. The demo computes an exact largest such set for small $N$ and shows its size grows like $N^{1-c_k}$ rather than linearly.

Context

For every fixed intersective integer polynomial $h$ of degree $k\ge2$ with positive leading coefficient, a subset of $\{1,\ldots,N\}$ avoiding nonzero values $h(1),h(2),\ldots$ as differences has size $O_h(N^{1-c_k})$, with $c_k>0$ depending only on degree. Here intersective means having a root modulo every modulus. For prime arguments, a power saving also holds when $h$ has a unit root modulo every modulus, with exponent allowed to depend on $h$.

Relevance

Gives the first power saving for polynomial-difference-free sets in general degree, improving on sub-polynomial bounds, and separates the ordinary intersective case from the prime-argument case where a unit root is available.
