The hypercube Ramsey conjecture (conceptual, result 171)

Compares the random-coloring, improved lower, and linear upper bounds for the two-color Ramsey number of the (n)-dimensional cube (Q_n), whose vertex count is (2^n).

Context

Resolves the Burr–Erdős hypercube Ramsey conjecture: the two-color Ramsey number of the (n)-dimensional cube (Q_n) is (\Theta(2^n)). Thus every red-blue coloring of the edges of a complete graph on (C2^n) vertices, for an absolute constant (C), contains a monochromatic copy of (Q_n). The cube (Q_n) has (2^n) vertices and (n2^{n-1}) edges.

Relevance

This places the hypercube in the linear Ramsey regime: a graph with exponentially many vertices has Ramsey number only linear in its size, unlike worst-case graphs whose Ramsey numbers are exponential in the number of vertices. The result separates sparse-graph Ramsey theory from dense extremal constructions and supports absorption and sprinkling methods for subgraph Ramsey problems.

Conceptual model: the random-coloring bound grows like (2^{n/2}), while the theorem places the Ramsey number between (c2^n) and (C2^n). The gap between the two parallel lines is the constant factor; changing (c) and (C) rescales the admissible interval without changing the linear order.
