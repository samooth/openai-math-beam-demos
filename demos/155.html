A counterexample to periodic tiling in dimension three (conceptual, result 155)

A finite translational tile in $\mathbb Z^3$ tiles space but admits no fully periodic tiling. The demo tests candidate period vectors on a visible patch of a tiling and contrasts a periodic tiling with the aperiodic counterexample.

Context

Constructs a finite translational tile in $\mathbb Z^3$ that tiles space but admits no fully periodic tiling, disproving the periodic tiling conjecture in the smallest possible lattice dimension. Its unit-cube thickening gives the same counterexample in $\mathbb R^3$, even with arbitrary real translation vectors.

Relevance

Identifies dimension three as the threshold where translational tilings need not be periodic. It separates the existence of a tiling from the existence of a periodic tiling, guides the study of aperiodic translational tiles, and affects decidability and structural questions for tiling problems.
