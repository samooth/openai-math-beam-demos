The Euclidean plane is not five-colorable (conceptual, result 158)

Explores the Hadwiger–Nelson chromatic number: a hexagonal 7-coloring of the plane, the new lower bound excluding 5 colors, and a draggable unit-distance probe.

Context

Proves that every five-coloring of the Euclidean plane has a monochromatic pair at distance one, with no restriction on the color classes. This advances the Hadwiger–Nelson problem: together with the classical seven-coloring, only six and seven remain possible chromatic numbers of the plane.

Relevance

The chromatic number of the plane is narrowed to (\{6,7\}): five colors are impossible even for arbitrary, non-measurable color classes. The proof transfers an arbitrary proper coloring to a weak measurable condition and then builds a geometric obstruction, showing that no regularity or measurability hypothesis is needed.
