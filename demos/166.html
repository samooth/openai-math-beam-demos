The higher-dimensional Erdős distinct-distances conjecture (conceptual, result 166)

Samples n points in (\mathbb R^d), counts distinct Euclidean distances, and compares the count with the optimal order (n^{2/d}).

Context

For every fixed integer \(d\ge 3\), every set of \(n\ge 2\) distinct points in \(\mathbb R^d\) determines at least \(c_d n^{2/d}\) distinct distances, with \(c_d>0) depending only on dimension. The exponent (2/d) is optimal: the integer grid (\{1,\dots,t\}^d) has (t^d) points, and its squared distances are integers between (1) and (d(t-1)^2), giving (O(t^2)=O(n^{2/d})) distinct distances. Thus the theorem resolves the higher-dimensional Erdős distinct-distances conjecture with a constant factor.

Relevance

This gives the first constant-factor bound at the conjectured exponent (2/d) in all dimensions (d\ge 3), rather than a bound with a subpolynomial or exponent loss. It separates the higher-dimensional regime from the planar problem, where the conjectured order is different, and shows that sets with very few distinct distances must be strongly concentrated in a grid-like way.
