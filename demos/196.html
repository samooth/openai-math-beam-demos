A counterexample to Kaplansky’s zero-divisor conjecture (conceptual, result 196)

Builds a small analogue of the cancellation mechanism: finite sets $A,B$, label sets with odd intersections, and matchings produce a graph on $A\times B$ whose connected components all have even size, so the product $\alpha\beta$ vanishes in $\mathbb F_2[G]$.

Context

Kaplansky’s zero-divisor conjecture asserts that $K[G]$ is a domain whenever $K$ is a field and $G$ is torsion-free. Result 196 disproves it over $\mathbb F_2$: there is a finitely presented torsion-free group $G$ and nonzero $\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb F_2[G]$ with $\alpha\beta=0$. The construction uses two immersed graphs $\Gamma_A,\Gamma_B$ on vertex sets $A,B$; their outgoing label sets $S_x,S_y\subset T$ are prescribed so that $|S_x\cap S_y|$ is odd for every $x\in A$, $y\in B$. The factors are $\alpha=\sum_{x\in A'}g_x$ and $\beta=\sum_{y\in B'}h_y^{-1}$ in $\mathbb F_2[G]$. Edges of a common label $t$ connect $(x,y)$ to the $t$-successors, preserving the product $g_xh_y^{-1}$; since every vertex has odd degree $|S_x\cap S_y|$, each connected component of this graph has even cardinality, and its contributions cancel in characteristic two.

Relevance

Shows that torsion-freeness alone does not make a group algebra a domain: the unique-product property can fail in a way that produces genuine zero divisors, not merely nontrivial units. The two safeguards are that each factor is nonzero (root separation) and that $G$ is torsion-free (asphericity of the coned complex $X$, giving a finite two-dimensional classifying space). This demo isolates the cancellation half of the argument in a finite, computable setting.
