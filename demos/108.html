A cubic permanent–determinant lower bound (conceptual, result 108)

The border determinantal complexity of the (m\times m) permanent over (\mathbb{C}) is (\Omega(m^3)). For (m\ge 1408), every affine-linear determinant representation, including coefficientwise limits, has size (n\ge m^3/(5529600e)). Slide the matrix size and a proposed representation size to test feasibility; vary the smooth initial form parameters (r,d) for the supporting bound ((r-1)(d-1)/(4e)).

Context

Proves an \(\Omega(m^3)\) lower bound for the border determinantal complexity of the \(m\times m\) permanent over \(\mathbb{C}\). Even coefficientwise limits of determinants of affine-linear matrices require matrix size at least \(cn^3\) (renaming \(m\)) for an absolute \(c>0) and all sufficiently large (m); the same bound holds for exact representations. For (m\ge 1408), the formalization gives the explicit estimate (n\ge m^3/(5529600e)). A supporting theorem applies to a polynomial in (d\ge 2) variables whose first nonzero homogeneous Taylor term has degree (r\ge 2) and has no nonzero singular zero: its exact and border determinantal sizes are at least ((r-1)(d-1)/(4e)). Algebraic-branching-program consequences are outside these statements.

Relevance

This is the first cubic lower bound for the permanent in the determinant model, including border (limit) representations, so it also rules out small exact determinantal expressions. It separates the permanent from determinant by a polynomial gap and constrains algebraic complexity measures tied to determinants. The smooth-initial-form estimate shows how a nondegenerate lowest-degree part alone forces a linear-in-((r-1)(d-1)) determinant-size barrier.
