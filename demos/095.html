Hyperbolicity cones without semidefinite lifts (conceptual, result 095)

A homogeneous polynomial is hyperbolic when a directional perturbation has only real roots; its hyperbolicity cone is the region where those roots are nonnegative. The generalized Lax conjecture says every such cone is spectrahedral, and the Projected Lax conjecture allows a finite semidefinite lift. This result shows some cones have no finite affine semidefinite lift at all, while a nonspectrahedral 23-variable cone still admits an exact lift.

Context

Proves that not every hyperbolicity cone is a spectrahedral shadow: some closed hyperbolicity cones admit no finite affine semidefinite lift, even with arbitrary real coefficients and any finite number of auxiliary variables. This disproves the Projected Lax Conjecture and hence the generalized Lax conjecture. A companion constructs a homogeneous polynomial of degree 16 in 23 real variables whose hyperbolicity cone has no finite homogeneous real symmetric linear matrix inequality representation, disproving the geometric generalized Lax conjecture; another companion constructs an exact semidefinite lift of that same cone with a homogeneous real symmetric pencil of size 100 and 307 auxiliary variables.

Relevance

Determines which convex cones arising from hyperbolic polynomials can be represented by semidefinite programs. It separates spectrahedral cones, spectrahedral shadows, and genuine hyperbolicity cones, limiting SDP modeling power and clarifying the boundary between polynomial-based and matrix-based convexity. The liftable nonspectrahedral example shows that auxiliary variables can restore an SDP representation even when no representation exists in the original coordinates.
