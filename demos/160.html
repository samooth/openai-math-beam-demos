Superexponential van der Waerden numbers (conceptual, result 160)

Colors an initial interval with (r) colors, searches for monochromatic (k)-term arithmetic progressions, and compares the observed progression-free length with the superexponential lower bound (k^{c k\lfloor\log_2 r\rfloor}).

Context

Resolves Erdős's superexponential-growth question for van der Waerden numbers. If \(W_r(k)\) is the least interval length forcing a monochromatic \(k\)-term progression in every \(r\)-coloring, then \(W_r(k)>k^{c k\lfloor\log_2 r\rfloor}\) for an absolute \(c>0), all (r\ge 2) and sufficiently large (k), uniformly in (r). In particular, (W_r(k)^{1/k}\to\infty) for each fixed (r).

Relevance

Places all two-or-more colorings in the superexponential regime, giving a quantitative positive resolution of Erdős's question and distinguishing growth in progression length from growth in the number of colors. The uniformity in (r) shows that the superexponential limit is not an artifact of fixing the number of colors.

Conceptual model: a random coloring of a short interval almost always contains monochromatic progressions, while the theorem guarantees a progression-free coloring exists up to length (k^{c k\lfloor\log_2 r\rfloor}). The lower curve is the proved avoidable length; the marked strip shows the actual coloring and any detected progressions.
