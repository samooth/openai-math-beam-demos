Stable-coordinate counterexample (conceptual, result 049)

An explicit degree-five polynomial (F\in\mathbb C[x_1,\dots,x_n]) is not a coordinate for (n\ge 4), yet becomes a coordinate after adjoining one variable. Every fiber is affine ((n-1))-space, but no fiber is rectifiable by an ambient automorphism. Toggle the dimension and adjoining to compare the stable and unstable coordinate statuses.

Context

Disproves the stable coordinate conjecture in four variables: a polynomial can become a coordinate after adjoining variables without already being one. The same construction gives, for every (n\ge 4), a polynomial (F\in\mathbb C[x_1,\ldots,x_n]) with quotient (\mathbb C[x_1,\ldots,x_n]/(F)\cong \mathbb C[y_1,\ldots,y_{n-1}]) that is not a coordinate. Every fiber is isomorphic to affine ((n-1))-space, yet none can be carried to a coordinate hyperplane by an ambient polynomial automorphism, also disproving the Abhyankar--Sathaye conjecture for affine-space fibers. A companion gives (n-1) commuting locally nilpotent derivations whose common kernel is generated by (F).

Relevance

Shows that quotient type alone does not determine coordinate status: affine-space fibers need not be rectifiable, and stability under adjoining variables is not automatic. This separates the stable coordinate and Abhyankar--Sathaye conjectures from the geometry of individual fibers and motivates the search for genuinely global, automorphism-invariant obstructions.
