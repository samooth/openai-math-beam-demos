The sharp simplex conjecture for isotropic constants (conceptual, result 101)

Simplices uniquely maximize the isotropic constant among convex bodies in every dimension. Slide the dimension to compare the isotropic constants of a simplex, a cube, and a ball, and watch the simplex pull away as dimension grows.

Context

Resolves the strong isotropic constant conjecture: among all convex bodies in R^n, simplices uniquely maximize the isotropic constant. The companion result gives a sharp entropy lower bound for log-concave probability densities, with equality exactly for invertible affine images of products of one-sided exponential laws. Together these identify the extremal geometries and distributions behind concentration, volume ratio, and isoperimetric inequalities in high dimension.

Relevance

The isotropic constant controls the best constants in the KLS and thin-shell conjectures, randomized volume algorithms, and high-dimensional concentration. Knowing that simplices are the worst case fixes the dimensional dependence of these estimates. The entropy bound gives the extremal log-concave distributions for information-theoretic inequalities and sharpens the link between convex geometry and probability.
