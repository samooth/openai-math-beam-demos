Rokhlin's multiple-mixing problem (conceptual, result 145)

Proves that every invertible mixing probability-preserving transformation is mixing of all finite orders, resolving Rokhlin's multiple-mixing problem for a single transformation. Correlations among any finite collection of measurable sets converge to the product of their measures whenever all pairwise time separations diverge.

Context

Result 145 proves Rokhlin's multiple-mixing conjecture for one transformation: if an invertible probability-preserving map (T) is mixing, then it is mixing of every finite order. Equivalently, for measurable sets (A_1,\dots,A_k) and times (t_i) with all successive gaps (t_{i+1}-t_i\to\infty), one has (\mu(T^{-t_1}A_1\cap\dots\cap T^{-t_k}A_k)\to\prod_{i=1}^k\mu(A_i)). The demo illustrates this decay of multi-set correlations for the doubling map (x\mapsto 2x\bmod 1), a canonical exact mixing system.

Relevance

Multiple mixing is the natural higher-order analogue of mixing and underlies pointwise multiple ergodic averages, joinings, and higher-order recurrence. Establishing it for an arbitrary single mixing transformation removes the need for stronger hypotheses (such as (K) or Bernoulli) in many arguments and shows that pairwise decorrelation propagates to arbitrary finite collections of observables.
