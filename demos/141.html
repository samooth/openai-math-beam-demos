The existential theory of the reals and existential–universal sentences in the counting hierarchy (conceptual, result 141)

Proves that the existential theory of the reals lies in the counting hierarchy. More generally, truth of existential–universal real sentences can be decided at one fixed level of that hierarchy, even when their integer polynomials are specified by arithmetic circuits.

Context

The existential theory of the reals asks whether a sentence (\exists x_1,\dots,x_n\in\mathbb R) with polynomial inequalities has a solution. Result 141 proves that this decision problem lies in the counting hierarchy (\mathrm{CH}), improving the classical PSPACE upper bound. More generally, truth of existential–universal real sentences (\exists x\forall yP(x,y)) with real variables can be decided at one fixed level of (\mathrm{CH}), even when the defining integer polynomials are specified compactly by arithmetic circuits rather than expanded coefficient lists. Thus real quantifier elimination and alternation can be placed inside a counting-based hierarchy, with the circuit representation not increasing the hierarchy level.

Relevance

This separates the complexity of real-number decision problems from full PSPACE and ties them to counting classes such as (\mathrm{PP}) and its relativized levels. It shows that existential–universal real geometry has a fixed counting-hierarchy cost, including for compressed polynomial representations, guiding reductions from geometry, optimization, and verification problems and clarifying the boundary between algebraic and counting complexity.
