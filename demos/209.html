Integral counterexamples to Gersten’s conjecture (conceptual, result 209)

Gersten’s conjecture predicts that for a Noetherian regular local domain $A$ with fraction field $F$, the map $K_n(A)\to K_n(F)$ is injective. This demo explores the ramified mixed-characteristic counterexamples: two-dimensional regular local rings for which an integral $K$-class vanishes over the fraction field.

Context

For a regular local domain $A$ with fraction field $F$, Gersten’s conjecture asserts exactness of the augmented residue complex, whose first assertion is generic injectivity: a class that vanishes in $K_n(F)$ must already vanish in $K_n(A)$. Result 209 disproves this in the unrestricted integral setting. In degree $5$, take $V$ the ring of integers in the unramified extension of $\mathbb Q_5$ of degree $8$ and $A=(V[x,y]/(5+x^4+y^4))_{(5,x,y)}$. Then $A$ is regular local of dimension $2$, its maximal ideal is $(x,y)$, and $5\in (x,y)^4$. The kernel $\ker(K_5(A)\to K_5(\operatorname{Frac}A))$ is nonzero. A companion degree-$3$ counterexample uses the cyclotomic quintic model over $\mathbb Q_5(\zeta)$. Positive theorems of Quillen, Panin, Gillet–Levine, Geisser–Levine, and Skalit require unramified or smooth hypotheses, which exclude $5\in\mathfrak m^4$.

Relevance

Settles the unrestricted integral form of Gersten’s conjecture negatively while leaving the finite-coefficient, unramified, and smooth cases intact. The detection branch of the proof uses a Picard test on an arithmetic surface: four specializations on the Fermat quartic map to $\pm Q$ on a genus-one curve, and their weighted sum $4Q$ is nonzero modulo $5$. The demo lets you vary the ramification depth and Bott signs to see when the obstruction survives.
