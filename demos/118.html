Unbounded bin-packing gaps and the modified integer round-up conjecture (conceptual, result 118)

Disproves the modified integer round-up conjecture of Scheithauer and Terno: the integral bin-packing optimum can exceed its configuration linear-programming value by an arbitrarily large additive constant. Approximating the optimum within any fixed additive constant is also NP-hard, even when every item exceeds $1/6$ and each bin holds at most five items.

Context

For every integer $c\ge 0$, there is a finite rational bin-packing instance $I$ and an integer $B$ such that every item size exceeds $1/6$, $\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{LP}}(I)=B$, and $\mathrm{OPT}(I)>B+c$. The same holds for the stronger configuration LP whose patterns are subsets of individual item copies, so the modified integer round-up bound $\mathrm{OPT}(I)\le \lceil \mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{LP}}(I)\rceil+1$ fails. Moreover, for every fixed $c\ge 0$, distinguishing instances that pack into $B$ bins from those needing more than $B+c$ bins is NP-hard; hence no deterministic polynomial-time algorithm can guarantee $\mathrm{OPT}(I)+C$ bins for an absolute constant $C$ unless $\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}$. Since every item exceeds $1/6$, each feasible bin contains at most five items.

Relevance

This settles the constant-additive approximation question for bin packing in the negative and refutes the modified integer round-up conjecture. It shows that configuration-LP rounding cannot by itself yield a fixed additive approximation, even in the restricted regime of small bin cardinality. The result is compatible with logarithmic additive upper bounds obtained by grouping and discrepancy-based rounding, because the constructed instances grow rapidly with the prescribed gap.
