A counterexample to Kurosh's division-ring problem (conceptual, result 201)

Kurosh asked whether a division ring algebraic over its center is locally finite. Result 201 answers no: there is a countable characteristic-zero division ring (D=F[x,y]), algebraic over its center (F), with ([D:F]=\infty).

Context

A division ring (D) is algebraic over its center (F=Z(D)) if every element satisfies a nonzero polynomial in (F[t]). It is locally finite if ([F(S):F]<\infty) for every finite (S\subseteq D). Result 201 constructs a countable characteristic-zero division ring (D=F[x,y]=F(x,y)) in which every element is algebraic over (F), yet ([D:F]=\infty). Thus algebraicity over the center does not imply local finiteness. The construction uses element-dependent polynomial degrees: there is no uniform degree bound, so the positive theorems of Jacobson and Kaplansky (which require bounded degree or a polynomial identity) do not apply. The visualization contrasts a fixed uniform degree bound (D), which forces dimension at most (D^2) over the center, with an unbounded schedule of relations, whose dimensions can grow without bound.

Relevance

This settles Kurosh's 1941 problem negatively and separates algebraicity from finite generation/dimension in noncommutative algebra. It clarifies that countability and element-dependent relations are essential for the counterexample, and it complements positive results: Kaplansky's theorem for PI algebras, Jacobson's bounded-degree theorem, and Bell–Drensky–Sharifi's (D^2) bound under a uniform degree. The demo illustrates the conceptual gap between a uniform degree bound (finite dimension) and an unbounded, scheduled sequence of algebraic relations (unbounded dimension).
