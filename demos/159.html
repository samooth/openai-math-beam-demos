Erdős’s reciprocal-sum conjecture and quasipolynomial Szemerédi bounds (conceptual, result 159)

Builds subsets of (\{1,\dots,N\}), detects arithmetic progressions, and compares their size and reciprocal sum with the quasipolynomial Szemerédi bound (C_k N\exp[-c_k(\log N)^{\varepsilon_k}]).

Context

Erdős’s reciprocal-sum conjecture asks whether every set of positive integers with divergent reciprocal sum contains arithmetic progressions of every finite length. Result 159 proves this: for every (k\ge 3), such a set contains a nonconstant (k)-term progression. Quantitatively, every subset of (\{1,\ldots,N\}) with no nonconstant (k)-term progression has size at most (C_k N\exp[-c_k(\log N)^{\varepsilon_k}]), with positive constants depending only on (k).

Relevance

This turns a density condition into a structural conclusion: enough additive mass in the reciprocals forces additive structure. The quasipolynomial bound improves the quantitative side of Szemerédi’s theorem, showing progression-free sets must be quasipolynomially sparse, while the reciprocal-sum theorem gives a natural divergence criterion for the existence of progressions in infinite sets.
