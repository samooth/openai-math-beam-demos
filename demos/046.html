Counterexamples to Shafarevich holomorphic convexity (conceptual, result 046)

Shafarevich asked whether the universal cover of every smooth connected projective complex variety is holomorphically convex. Toggle the scenarios to compare the conjectured behavior with the two counterexamples: a surface whose cover contains an infinite Nori string of rational curves, and a fourfold with large fundamental group whose cover has no compact subvarieties but is not Stein.

Context

Constructs a smooth projective complex fourfold with large fundamental group whose universal cover contains no positive-dimensional compact analytic subvariety but is neither Stein nor holomorphically convex. A separate smooth projective complex surface already disproves unrestricted Shafarevich holomorphic convexity in dimension two: its universal cover contains a closed connected noncompact locally finite union of compact rational curves (an infinite Nori string), so the holomorphic hull of a point is noncompact.

Relevance

Shows that holomorphic convexity can fail in the smallest possible complex dimension and also under the large-fundamental-group hypothesis. The surface obstruction is a connected curve with trivial component fundamental groups but infinite image of its nodal incidence graph; the fourfold obstruction is topological, coming from a rank-three lattice cover of a simple abelian surface with nonzero third homology, which no Stein surface can have.
