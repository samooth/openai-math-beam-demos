Ostmann's inverse Goldbach conjecture

Can the set of primes (up to finite modification) be written as a sumset A+B with A,B subsets of the nonnegative integers, each with at least two elements? The theorem says no.

Context

Ostmann's inverse Goldbach conjecture asks whether the primes are additively decomposable: whether there exist A,B ⊆ ℤ_{\ge 0}, |A|,|B| ≥ 2, such that A+B equals the primes (possibly after finitely many insertions/deletions). The result proves that no finite modification of the primes can be so expressed; the primes are additively indecomposable.

Relevance

This settles a classical additive-combinatorics question about the primes: unlike the integers (which are 1+ℕ), the primes cannot be factored as a sum of two substantial sets, even permitting finite errors. The checker below verifies indecomposability by exhaustive search for small N.
