A counterexample to the small Cohen–Macaulay module conjecture (conceptual, result 195)

A small Cohen–Macaulay module over a Noetherian local ring ((R,\mathfrak m)) is a nonzero finitely generated (R)-module (M) with (\depth_R M=\dim R). Result 195 produces a three-dimensional complete Noetherian normal local domain over (\mathbb C) with no such module.

Context

The domain form of the small Cohen–Macaulay module conjecture asked whether every complete Noetherian local domain has a nonzero finitely generated maximal Cohen–Macaulay module. Result 195 answers negatively: for the surface-cone ring \(R(X,H)\), if a small Cohen–Macaulay module existed then Theorem 1.2 would require \(K_X^2-2c_2(X)\le 6H^2\). The explicit Hirzebruch–Kummer cover with prime \(n=7\) has \(K_X^2-2c_2(X)=7^3(7^2-10)=13377\) and \(H^2=5\cdot 7^3=1715\), hence \(6H^2=10290\). Since \(13377>10290), no such module exists.

Relevance

This separates finite modules from big Cohen–Macaulay modules and module-finite extensions: big Cohen–Macaulay algebras exist in equal characteristic, while finite generation can fail. It also gives a counterexample essentially of finite type over (\mathbb C), resolving the affine-local existence problem in dimension three over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero.

The obstruction applies when \(n^3(n^2-10)>30n^3\), i.e. \(n^2>40). For (n=7) this gives the counterexample; for (n=3,5) the inequality does not obstruct.
