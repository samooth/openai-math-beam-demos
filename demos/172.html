Classification of finite Euclidean Ramsey configurations (conceptual, result 172)

Tests finite point configurations on a circle against the Euclidean Ramsey criterion: sphericity, affine span, transitivity, and quadratic independence of the augmented coordinate tensor.

Context

A finite set (A\subset\mathbb R^d) is Ramsey if every finite coloring of some high-dimensional Euclidean space contains a monochromatic congruent copy of (A) at the original scale. Result 172 gives a necessary and sufficient algebraic condition: with (p_i=(1,a_i)), coordinate field (F), and (B=F\otimes_{\mathbb Q}F), (A) is Ramsey iff there is (P\in\operatorname{Mat}_{d+1}(B)) with ((p_i\otimes 1)^T P(1\otimes p_i)=0) and (m_F(P_{\alpha\beta})=\delta_{\alpha\beta}). Sphericity follows by applying (m_F), but the tensor condition detects the stronger Ramsey property. The theorem also disproves the Leader--Russell--Walters conjecture that every Ramsey set embeds in a finite transitive set: cyclic kites are Ramsey without being subtransitive, while nine algebraically independent circle points are not Ramsey.

Relevance

This turns Euclidean Ramsey theory from a list of special constructions into an exact algebraic classification. It separates sphericity from Ramseyness, explains why transitive and quadratically independent spherical sets are Ramsey, and gives explicit obstructions for generic circle configurations. The criterion is over the exact coordinate field, so numerical approximation alone cannot decide Ramseyness; the demo illustrates the structural cases rather than computing the tensor directly.
