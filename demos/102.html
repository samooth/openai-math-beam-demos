The Unique Games Conjecture and optimal approximation thresholds (conceptual, result 102)

Khot's Unique Games Conjecture is proved: for every fixed (0<\varepsilon,\delta<1/2\), there is a deterministic polynomial-time reduction from 3SAT to Unique Games with completeness at least \(1-\varepsilon\) and soundness at most \(\delta\). Direct reductions also give optimal NP-hardness thresholds for Max-Cut, Vertex Cover, Min-UnCut, and directed feedback vertex set.

Context

The Unique Games conjecture asks for hardness of distinguishing nearly satisfiable unique games from games of very small value. The proved result gives, for every fixed (0<\varepsilon,\delta<1/2\), a deterministic polynomial-time reduction from binary 3SAT to nonempty unweighted simple bipartite unique games. Satisfiable inputs have value at least \(1-\varepsilon\) and unsatisfiable inputs have value at most \(\delta\). The alphabet is a fixed \(\mathbb F_2^s\) depending only on the errors, and every constraint is a translation. Independent direct reductions establish NP-hardness, on unweighted graphs, of approximation beyond the Goemans–Williamson ratio for Max-Cut, below factor two for Vertex Cover, and within any fixed constant factor for Min-UnCut and directed feedback vertex set.

Relevance

This settles the Unique Games Conjecture and confirms the optimal approximation thresholds predicted by it. It shows that no polynomial-time algorithm can beat the Goemans–Williamson constant for Max-Cut, achieve factor below two for Vertex Cover, or obtain any fixed constant-factor approximation for Min-UnCut or directed feedback vertex set, unless P=NP. The reductions are gap reductions from 3SAT, so the hardness is unconditional relative to NP.
