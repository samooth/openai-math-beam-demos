The complexity of Weisfeiler–Leman refinement (conceptual, result 133)

For sufficiently large fixed (k), deciding (k)-dimensional Weisfeiler–Leman equivalence requires (n^{\Omega(k)}) deterministic time in the stated sequential adjacency-matrix models, for both joint and separate refinement. When the dimension is part of the input, joint equivalence is EXPTIME-complete even on subcubic graphs, and deciding whether refinement identifies a graph is also EXPTIME-complete.

Context

Result 133 gives unconditional deterministic time lower bounds for \(k\)-dimensional Weisfeiler–Leman equivalence: for every sufficiently large fixed \(k\), any deciding algorithm in the stated multitape Turing-machine or sequential logarithmic-word RAM model needs at least \(n^{ck}\) worst-case time for all sufficiently large graph orders \(n\), with one absolute \(c>0). The bound holds for both joint and separate replacement conventions and remains valid for explicit adjacency-matrix inputs restricted to simple connected uncolored graphs of diameter at most two. The parity-lift construction detects a successful choice by round two under joint refinement and round one under separate refinement, yielding fixed-dimension exclusions. When (k\ge 2) is supplied in binary and graphs are encoded by explicit adjacency matrices, joint equivalence is EXPTIME-complete, even restricted to connected simple uncolored graphs of equal positive order and maximum degree at most three. Deciding whether Weisfeiler–Leman refinement of a supplied dimension identifies a graph is also EXPTIME-complete, quantifying over every comparison graph and including both the exponential-time upper bound and hardness.

Relevance

This shows that Weisfeiler–Leman equivalence is not solvable in polynomial time for fixed but large dimension, since the required degree grows with (k), and becomes EXPTIME-complete when the dimension is part of the input. It therefore separates bounded-dimension refinement from efficient graph isomorphism-style procedures at sufficiently high dimension and establishes the hardness of graph identification by refinement, with applications to graph-isomorphism lower bounds and complexity classification of canonical forms.
