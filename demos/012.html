Largest prime factors of consecutive integers

For each n, let P+(n) be the largest prime factor of n. The plot shows log P+(n) versus log P+(n+1); the shaded triangle is where P+(n) < P+(n+1).

Context

Resolves the Erdős–Pomerance joint Dickman conjecture: for 0<a,b<1, the natural density of integers n with P+(n) ≤ n^a and P+(n+1) ≤ n^b tends to ρ(1/a) ρ(1/b), where ρ is the Dickman function. Hence the logarithmic sizes of the largest prime factors of n and n+1 are asymptotically independent in ordinary natural density.

Relevance

In particular, the integers satisfying P+(n) < P+(n+1) have density 1/2. The scatter below colors points by this inequality; as N grows the proportion in the shaded triangle approaches 1/2.
