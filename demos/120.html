Almost-linear-time exact matching in general graphs (conceptual, result 120)

A randomized algorithm finds an exact maximum-cardinality matching in any simple undirected graph in $(n+m)^{1+o(1)}$ word time, with success probability at least $2/3$. The time bound holds on every computation path. The same guarantees apply to finding a spanning subgraph with prescribed admissible vertex degrees, or deciding that none exists.

Context

The algorithm works for every simple undirected graph on $n$ vertices and $m$ edges, returning a maximum-cardinality matching exactly. Its running time is $(n+m)^{1+o(1)}$ word operations, and the bound holds on every computation path rather than only in expectation. The success probability is at least $2/3$, and independent repetitions raise it to $1-(1/3)^r$. The same framework also finds a spanning subgraph satisfying prescribed admissible vertex degrees, or correctly decides that no such subgraph exists.

Relevance

This breaks the long-standing near-cubic barrier for exact maximum matching in general graphs, bringing it to almost-linear time. It makes exact matching and degree-constrained subgraph problems practical at large scale, with predictable worst-case time and simple amplification of the success probability.
