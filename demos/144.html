Banach's simple Lebesgue-spectrum problem (conceptual, result 144)

A $C^\infty$ volume-preserving diffeomorphism of the standard-volume three-torus has simple Lebesgue spectrum on the entire complex mean-zero $L^2$ space: the bilateral iterates of one real observable form an orthonormal basis of that space.

Context

Banach's simple Lebesgue-spectrum problem asks for a probability-preserving transformation whose Koopman operator on the entire orthogonal complement of the constants is unitarily equivalent to multiplication by $w$ on $L^2(S^1,m)$. Result 144 realizes this in smooth dynamics: there exist a $C^\infty$ volume-preserving diffeomorphism $T:\mathbb T^3\to\mathbb T^3$ and a real $f\in L^2_0(\mathbb T^3,\mu)$ such that $\{f\circ T^n:n\in\mathbb Z\}$ is an orthonormal basis of $L^2_0(\mathbb T^3,\mu)$. Thus one cyclic vector spans the whole centered space, and the Koopman operator on that space has simple Lebesgue spectral type. The invariant measure is the standard smooth volume itself.

Relevance

This gives a smooth probability-preserving system with maximal spectral simplicity. It implies ergodicity and ordinary mixing; with the separate multiple-mixing theorem it yields mixing of every order. Rokhlin's finite-multiplicity entropy theorem gives Kolmogorov-Sinai entropy $h_\mu(T)=0$, and Pesin's entropy formula with volume preservation gives vanishing Lyapunov exponents at $\mu$-almost every point. It also distinguishes a full simple Lebesgue orbit from earlier constructions that had only a Lebesgue component or higher multiplicity.
