A power improvement in the Heilbronn triangle problem (conceptual, result 191)

For \(n\) points in the unit square, Heilbronn's problem asks how large the smallest determined triangle can be made. Result 191 gives a construction with every triangle of area at least \(n^{-2+c}\) for a fixed \(c>0).

Context

For every sufficiently large \(n\), there exist \(n\) points in the unit square such that every triangle determined by three of them has area at least \(n^{-2+c}\) for one absolute \(c>0). This disproves the conjectured almost-(n^{-2}) upper bound in Heilbronn's triangle problem, which asks how large the smallest determined triangle can be.

Relevance

The construction creates a power gap above the \(n^{-2}\) scale: the best possible minimum triangle area is not of order \(n^{-2+\varepsilon}\) for every \(\varepsilon>0). The demo computes the minimum triangle area for several point families and compares it with (n^{-2}) and (n^{-2+c}).
