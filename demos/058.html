The Kollár–Pardon universal-cover conjecture (conceptual, result 058)

For a connected normal projective variety (X), its universal cover (\widetilde X) admits a semialgebraic projective-open presentation iff it is biholomorphic to (D\times\mathbb C^m\times F), with (D) a bounded symmetric domain and (F) simply connected, normal, and projective. Tune the three factors to see the classification and its geometric consequences.

Context

Proves the Kollár–Pardon conjecture: the semialgebraic universal covers of connected normal projective complex varieties are exactly products (D\times\mathbb C^m\times F), where (D) is a bounded symmetric domain, (m\ge 0), and (F) is simply connected, normal, and projective. The compact factor may be singular; retaining normal singularities is essential. The proof combines a bounded-domain/semialgebraic quotient construction with Campana's core and the virtual abelianity of fundamental groups of smooth compact special Kähler manifolds.

Relevance

Classifies universal covers that admit a finite real-algebraic description, separating hyperbolic (\(D\)), flat/affine (\(\mathbb C^m\)), and simply connected compact (\(F\)) geometry. It yields a Remmert reduction \(\widetilde X\to D\times\mathbb C^m\), Steinness iff \(F\) is a point, and quasi-projectivity of the universal cover iff the bounded symmetric factor is absent. In the smooth case, a variety covered by \(\mathbb C^n\) has a finite étale cover by an abelian variety, and Singer-type \(L^2\)-Betti vanishing follows when \(m>0).
