Compact counterexamples to bi-Lipschitz dimension reduction (conceptual, result 098)

Every infinite-dimensional real Banach space contains a compact doubling set admitting no bi-Lipschitz embedding into any finite-dimensional normed space. The doubling constant is universal, answering the Lang--Plaut problem negatively even for compact subsets of Hilbert space.

Context

Every infinite-dimensional real Banach space contains a compact doubling subset that admits no bi-Lipschitz embedding into any finite-dimensional real normed space. The doubling constant has a universal bound, independent of the ambient Banach space. This answers the Lang--Plaut problem negatively, even for compact subsets of Hilbert space. In the Hilbert case there is a doubling subset of real $\ell_2$ with doubling constant at most $76800$ and no finite-dimensional Euclidean embedding at any finite distortion.

Relevance

This obstructs universal finite-dimensional bi-Lipschitz dimension reduction for compact doubling metric spaces: no fixed target dimension can approximate all such sets with bounded distortion. It informs metric geometry, Banach space theory, and the limits of sketching and embedding methods for high- and infinite-dimensional data.
