A $C^1$ counterexample to Shub’s entropy conjecture (conceptual, result 151)

Constructs a noninvertible $C^1$ self-map of a compact smooth manifold with zero topological entropy but eigenvalue $2$ on second homology, disproving the homological entropy lower bound in $C^1$ regularity.

Context

Shub’s entropy conjecture proposes that a $C^1$ self-map $f:M\to M$ of a compact smooth manifold has topological entropy at least $\log\rho(f_*:H_*(M;\mathbb R)\to H_*(M;\mathbb R))$. Result 151 constructs a noninvertible $C^1$ self-map of a compact smooth manifold with zero topological entropy but eigenvalue $2$ on second homology. Thus homological growth need not force positive orbit complexity for merely $C^1$ maps. The bound is restored for $C^\infty$ maps by Yomdin’s theorem.

Relevance

This identifies regularity as the decisive threshold for homological entropy lower bounds. It shows that noninvertible $C^1$ dynamics can have exponential homology growth with zero topological entropy, separating algebraic (homological) invariants from orbit complexity. The counterexample guides where smoothness hypotheses are essential in entropy estimates and in reductions from topology to dynamics.
