A counterexample to the little finitistic-dimension conjecture (conceptual, result 198)

The little finitistic-dimension conjecture asks whether, over a fixed finite-dimensional algebra, the finite projective dimensions of finitely generated modules are bounded. This demo shows unbounded growth: for each $m\ge1$ there is a module $M_m$ with projective dimension at least $2m-2$.

Context

The little finitistic-dimension conjecture predicts that finitely generated modules of finite projective dimension over a fixed finite-dimensional algebra have bounded projective dimensions. The formalized counterexample is a finite-dimensional complex algebra with a finitely generated module of projective dimension at least $2m-2$, still finite, for every $m\ge1$. A separate formalized result gives a finite-dimensional complex algebra whose little and big finitistic dimensions are infinite on the left and zero on the right; injectives fail to generate its unbounded derived category on the left and generate it on the right.

Relevance

Disproves the little finitistic-dimension conjecture by exhibiting a fixed finite-dimensional complex algebra with finitely generated modules of arbitrarily large, yet finite, projective dimension. The related left/right asymmetry shows that injective generation of an unbounded derived category can fail on one side while holding on the other.
