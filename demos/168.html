Combinatorial invariance of Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomials (conceptual, result 168)

Compares two Bruhat intervals as graded posets. It tests whether their Hasse diagrams are order-isomorphic and displays the model Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomial attached to each interval shape.

Context

The combinatorial invariance conjecture asks whether a Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomial depends only on its Bruhat interval as an ordered set. Result 168 proves that every order isomorphism between Bruhat intervals in arbitrary Coxeter systems preserves the corresponding equal-parameter Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomial. The two intervals may come from different Coxeter systems.

Relevance

This shows that the polynomial is a function of the abstract interval poset, not of the ambient Coxeter system. It unifies calculations across different groups, permits poset-based algorithms and invariants, and reduces an algebraic object to finite combinatorics of Bruhat intervals.

Conceptual model: the Hasse diagrams show graded posets. When the two intervals are order-isomorphic, result 168 predicts identical Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomials; when they are not, the model allows different polynomials. Exact Coxeter-group computations are replaced here by poset-shape models.
