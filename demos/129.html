Exponential state costs for two-way automata (conceptual, result 129)

For two-way nondeterministic finite automata, complementation and determinization can require exponentially many states; the alphabet must grow with the automaton size. The same phenomenon occurs for two-way deterministic recognition of one-way nondeterministic languages.

Context

For every $n\ge4$, there is an explicit $n$-state two-way nondeterministic finite automaton whose complement requires at least $\frac12 2^{\lfloor (n-4)/127\rfloor}-1$ states. For the same relational languages and every $n\ge131$, every equivalent deterministic two-way automaton requires at least $\frac12 2^{\lfloor (n-4)/127\rfloor}$ states. Thus neither complementation nor determinization has a polynomial state bound uniform over alphabets. Separately, for every $h\ge2$ there is a one-way nondeterministic automaton with $h+3$ states whose language requires exponentially many states for two-way deterministic recognition: any deterministic recognizer with $s$ states satisfies $2^{\lfloor (h-2)/31\rfloor}\le 4(s+2)^2$, with the sharper factor $4(s+1)^2$ under one acceptance convention.

Relevance

This resolves the Sakoda--Sipser state-succinctness question over growing finite alphabets and rules out polynomial state bounds independent of alphabet size for both complementation and determinization. It shows that two-way nondeterminism is exponentially more state-efficient than two-way determinism or complementation, and that alphabet growth is essential to the lower bound.
