Goldfeld's conjecture

For quadratic twists of an elliptic curve over $\mathbb Q$, half have analytic rank $0$ and half rank $1$; higher ranks have density $0$ and the mean rank tends to $1/2$.

Context

Goldfeld's conjecture concerns the distribution of analytic ranks among the family of quadratic twists $E_d$ of a fixed elliptic curve $E/\mathbb Q$, ordered by $|d|$. It predicts that ranks $0$ and $1$ each occur with natural density $1/2$, that ranks $\ge 2$ have density $0$, and consequently the mean analytic rank tends to $1/2$. This result proves it for every elliptic curve over $\mathbb Q$.

Relevance

Together with the low-Selmer-corank BSD formula (result 002), this gives the full Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer formula for a density-one set of quadratic twists of every elliptic curve over $\mathbb Q$. It also settles the mean rank and the density of ranks $0,1$.
