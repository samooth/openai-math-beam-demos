The geometric case of the Erdős similarity conjecture (conceptual, result 084)

For every fixed (q\in(0,1)), constructs compact (E\subset[0,1]) of measure arbitrarily close to (1) containing no translated and nontrivially dilated copy of (\{q^n:n\ge1\}), with dilations of either sign. Tune the ratio (q), the removed measure, translation, and dilation; inspect the progression points and whether the chosen affine copy survives in (E).

Context

For every fixed (q\in(0,1)), constructs compact subsets of ([0,1]) with measure arbitrarily close to one containing no translated and nontrivially dilated copy of (\{q^n:n\ge1\}), with dilations of either sign. This resolves the geometric-progression case of the Erdős similarity conjecture for every ratio. The construction removes, in a controlled way, one point from each possible affine copy while keeping the total removed measure small, and both signs of the dilation are handled.

Relevance

The Erdős similarity conjecture asks whether every infinite set of reals can be avoided by a set of positive measure under translations and dilations; geometric progressions are a central test family. Resolving them for every ratio (q\in(0,1)) gives a concrete, high-measure obstruction example and clarifies the interplay between exponential decay, affine invariance, and measure-theoretic largeness. It also feeds the broader program of pattern-avoiding sets, Salem-type constructions, and the limits of measure-theoretic Ramsey phenomena.
