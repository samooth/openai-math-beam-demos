Cylinder coverings below the half-area bound (conceptual, result 100)

A regular tetrahedron is covered by finitely many cylinders with compact triangular perpendicular bases. The total base area is strictly below half the smallest orthogonal projection area, disproving Bang's half-area cylinder-covering bound.

Context

The half-area cylinder-covering conjecture predicts that any cylinder cover of a convex body has total perpendicular-base area at least half its smallest projection area. The construction covers the entire closed regular tetrahedron by finitely many cylinders with compact triangular bases whose total area is strictly less than that bound. For a small parameter (\varepsilon), the normalized area is (1/2-(13/6000)\varepsilon^2+O(\varepsilon^4)). By affine invariance, the same idea gives a counterexample to the directionwise normalized half-bound for every nondegenerate tetrahedron.

Relevance

This disproves Bang's half-area cylinder-covering bound and the stronger directionwise normalized conjecture in dimension three. It shows that finite angular covers can beat the classical half-area estimate, with consequences for covering, projection, and ruled-surface approximation problems. The companion approximation results handle ruled sets and radial sweeps by finite cylinders.
