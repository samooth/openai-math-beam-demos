A counterexample to the quadratic sensitivity conjecture (conceptual, result 132)

Constructs total Boolean functions with block sensitivity $\operatorname{bs}(f)\ge s(f)^\alpha$ for a fixed $\alpha>2$, disproving the quadratic strengthening of the Sensitivity Conjecture. Here $s(f)$ counts influential individual-bit flips, while block sensitivity allows disjoint groups of bits to change together.

Context

Result 132 disproves the quadratic strengthening of the Sensitivity Conjecture: for every constant $C$, there is a total Boolean function $f$ with $\operatorname{bs}(f)>Cs(f)^2$. Sensitivity $s(f)$ is the maximum over inputs $x$ of the number of coordinates $i$ for which flipping bit $i$ alone changes $f(x)$. Block sensitivity $\operatorname{bs}(f)$ is the maximum number of pairwise disjoint nonempty sets of coordinates whose simultaneous flip changes $f(x)$. Nisan and Szegedy suggested $\operatorname{bs}(f)\le s(f)^2$; Rubinstein, Virza, and Ambainis--Sun gave quadratic separations, while Huang's theorem (with Tal's refinement) gives the polynomial bound $\operatorname{bs}(f)\le O(s(f)^4)$. The construction uses nested predicates on a labelled tournament: at depth $d$, sensitivity grows by at most about $M$ per level, while disjoint sensitive blocks grow by about $2M^2+1$ per level, so their ratio grows like $2^d/[4(d+2)^2]$. An OR of $M$ copies balances values at $0$ and $1$.

Relevance

This removes the last plausible universal quadratic relation between two central complexity measures of Boolean functions. It implies that block sensitivity can be arbitrarily larger than the square of sensitivity, affecting translations between certificate complexity, decision-tree complexity, and polynomial degree. The construction also yields a fixed seed with $\operatorname{bs}(f,0)>s(f)^2$; iterating disjoint composition gives functions with $\operatorname{bs}(F_m,0)\ge s(F_m)^\alpha$ for a fixed $\alpha>2$.
