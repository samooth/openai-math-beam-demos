Counterexamples to conjectures of Auslander--Reiten, Tachikawa, and Nakayama (conceptual, result 199)

Finite-dimensional algebras over $k=\mathbb F_2(q,H_1,H_2)$ can have a nonprojective module $M$ with $\operatorname{Ext}^i(M,M\oplus A)=0$ for all $i>0$, while the algebra is self-injective or symmetric. Toggle the homological profile below to see which conjectures are contradicted and how the radical filtration supports the construction.

Context

Constructs finite-dimensional algebras over a characteristic-two rational-function field that disprove the Auslander--Reiten and Gorenstein-projective conjectures, and Tachikawa's second conjecture. An associated endomorphism algebra also disproves the classical, generalized and strong Nakayama conjectures, the Auslander--Gorenstein conjecture, and the Wakamatsu tilting conjecture. The counterexamples persist under every extension of the base field. The algebra has eight simple modules, radical fourth power nonzero, and a nonprojective Gorenstein-projective module with vanishing positive Ext.

Relevance

Shows that vanishing of positive Ext groups does not force projectivity for Artin algebras, even in the self-injective or symmetric case. It separates the Auslander--Reiten, Tachikawa, Gorenstein-projective, Nakayama, Auslander--Gorenstein, and Wakamatsu tilting conjectures, and exhibits the role of characteristic two, transcendental parameters, and radical depth in counterexamples.
