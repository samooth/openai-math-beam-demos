The second Kahn--Kalai conjecture (conceptual, result 176)

Studies when a fixed graph $H$ appears as a subgraph of the Erdős--Rényi random graph $G(n,p)$, comparing the sharp appearance threshold with the expectation threshold $p_{\mathrm E}(n,H)$.

Context

For a finite simple graph $H$ with $h\ge 1$ edges and at most $n$ vertices, the appearance threshold in $G(n,p)$ is the probability $p$ at which $H$ becomes likely to occur as a (not necessarily induced) subgraph. Result 176 proves the second Kahn--Kalai conjecture: this threshold is at most $Cp_{\mathrm E}(n,H)(1+\log_2 h)$ for a universal constant $C$. Here $p_{\mathrm E}(n,H)$ is the least density such that every subgraph $F\subseteq H$ has expected copy count at least $1/2$ in $G(n,p)$. Thus the threshold is controlled, up to a logarithmic factor in the number of edges, by the densest substructure of $H$.

Relevance

This resolves a central random-graph threshold problem and explains why thresholds are governed by densest subgraphs rather than by $H$ as a whole. It underlies phase transitions in random networks, motif appearance, and the general theory of Boolean functions and thresholds. The demo lets you choose a pattern $H$, vary $n$ and $p$, and see the sampled graph, the expectation threshold, the logarithmic bound, and whether an embedding of $H$ is present.
