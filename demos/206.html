Finite lattice representation and undecidability (conceptual, result 206)

A finite lattice is representable as the congruence lattice of a finite algebra iff it admits a finite nonempty colored-graph witness. The demo searches small witnesses for selected lattices.

Context

The finite lattice representation problem asks which finite lattices occur as full congruence lattices of finite algebras. Result 206 proves the colored-graph criterion: a finite nonempty lattice is representable exactly when it has a finite nonempty graph witness whose edge colors are lattice elements. The colors satisfy symmetry, ( \bot ) iff equality, a triangle inequality ( \mathrm{color}(x,y)\le \mathrm{color}(x,z)\sqcup \mathrm{color}(z,y) ), separation of non-comparable bounds, and a connectivity condition for seeds. The formalization covers this equivalence; the paper's undecidability and subgroup-interval conclusions lie outside it.

Relevance

This reframes representability as the existence of a finite combinatorial witness, connecting universal algebra with graph coloring and lattice theory. It also implies undecidability: no algorithm decides whether an arbitrary finite lattice has such a representation, or whether it is a full subgroup interval of a finite group. The interactive search below enumerates colored graphs on a small vertex set and checks the witness axioms for selected lattices.
