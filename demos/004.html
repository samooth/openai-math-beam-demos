Hilbert's tenth problem over ℚ

Enter an integer-coefficient polynomial and a bound N; brute-force search for integer triples (x,y,z) with |x|,|y|,|z| ≤ N where the polynomial vanishes.

Context

Hilbert's tenth problem asks for an algorithm deciding whether a Diophantine polynomial has a rational (equivalently, after clearing denominators, integer) zero. It is undecidable over ℤ (Matiyasevich) and, by this result, over ℚ: no algorithm can decide in general. The demo illustrates the only available general method — exhaustive search up to a finite bound — which is sound but never complete.

Relevance

Settles the rational case of Hilbert's tenth problem, removing the last major variant of the decision problem and confirming that Diophantine equations over ℚ are as computationally intractable as over ℤ.
