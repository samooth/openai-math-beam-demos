The Sobolev endpoint in Carleson's Schrödinger convergence problem (conceptual, result 080)

For (f\in H^s(\mathbb R^n)), the free Schrödinger evolution (e^{it\Delta}f) converges to (f) pointwise almost everywhere as (t\downarrow0) once (s\ge n/(2(n+1))). This resolves the sharp Sobolev endpoint of Carleson's problem, including the planar case (H^{1/3}). Tune dimension, regularity, and time.

Context

Proves almost-everywhere convergence (e^{it\Delta}f\to f) as (t\downarrow0) for every (f\in H^{n/(2(n+1))}(\mathbb R^n)) and every dimension (n\ge2). This attains the sharp Sobolev equality case of Carleson's Schrödinger convergence problem, including the planar endpoint (H^{1/3}). Relevance

Gives the sharp threshold (s(n)=n/(2(n+1))) for almost-everywhere pointwise recovery of Schrödinger data. In dimension two this is (H^{1/3}); the threshold rises toward (1/2) in high dimensions. It separates regularity regimes where pointwise convergence holds from those where maximal-function and counterexample obstructions remain, and it underpins dispersive PDE and harmonic-analysis arguments requiring precise control of Schrödinger solutions.
