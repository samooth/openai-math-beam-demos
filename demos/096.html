The Gaussian propeller conjecture (conceptual, result 096)

For a standard Gaussian in (\mathbb R^d), the sum over the cells of any finite measurable partition of (|\mathbb E[g\mathbf 1_{\text{cell}}]|^2) is at most (9/(8\pi)). In dimension at least two, three planar sectors of angle (2\pi/3), extended orthogonally, attain the bound.

Context

Proves that the sum of squared Gaussian first moments of any finite measurable partition is at most (9/(8\pi)). In dimension at least two, three planar sectors of angle (2\pi/3), extended orthogonally, attain the bound. Combined with the separate Unique Games theorem, this proves NP-hardness of improving the loss factor ((8\pi/9)(1-1/k)) for identity-target kernel clustering with fixed (k\ge 3) on rational centered positive semidefinite inputs.

Relevance

The sharp constant (9/(8\pi)) is the extremal value for Gaussian first-moment energy; the (120^\circ) propeller extremizers show how mass and direction align. Together with the separate Unique Games theorem, it yields NP-hardness of improving the loss factor ((8\pi/9)(1-1/k)) for identity-target kernel clustering with fixed (k\ge 3) on rational centered positive semidefinite inputs.
