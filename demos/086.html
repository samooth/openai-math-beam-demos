An L^3 bound for the trilinear Hilbert transform (conceptual, result 086)

The principal-value trilinear transform with shifts (x-t), (x-2t), (x-3t) is bounded from (L^3(R)^3) to (L^1(R)). It resolves the (L^3) exponent case of the standard conjecture for slopes 1, 2, 3.

Context

For Schwartz functions (f_1,f_2,f_3:\mathbb R\to\mathbb C), define (T(f_1,f_2,f_3)(x)=\operatorname{p.v.}\int_{\mathbb R} f_1(x-t)f_2(x-2t)f_3(x-3t)\frac{dt}{t}). The result proves (|T(f_1,f_2,f_3)|_{L^1(\mathbb R)}\le C\prod_{j=1}^3|f_j|_{L^3(\mathbb R)}), so (T) extends boundedly from (L^3(\mathbb R)^3) to (L^1(\mathbb R)). This is the (L^3\times L^3\times L^3\to L^1) case of the standard trilinear Hilbert transform conjecture for fixed slopes 1, 2, 3.

Relevance

The transform is a model multilinear singular integral. The proof must organize cancellation across scales while quadratic phases persist, since coefficients such as ((-1,3,-3,1)) annihilate constants, linear, and quadratic terms. The estimate underpins multilinear harmonic analysis and the study of multiple ergodic averages, where the same trilinear structure appears.
