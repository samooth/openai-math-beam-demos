Counterexamples to Sidorenko’s conjecture and the forcing conjecture (conceptual, result 161)

Studies a connected bipartite pattern (H) on (35) vertices and (66) edges, comparing its homomorphism density (t(H,G)) with the Sidorenko bound (t(K_2,G)^{66}) and testing whether matching these densities forces quasirandomness.

Context

Sidorenko’s conjecture predicts that every bipartite graph (H) has homomorphism density at least the host graph’s edge density raised to (|E(H)|): (t(H,G)\ge t(K_2,G)^{|E(H)|}). Relevance

This shows that bipartite subgraph density can be genuinely extremal and non-random: homomorphism counts need not be minimized by the random graph of the same edge density. It also separates density equalities from quasirandomness, clarifying which graph patterns genuinely force uniform edge distribution and which do not. The result reshapes extremal graph theory, graphon theory, and the study of quasirandomness.
