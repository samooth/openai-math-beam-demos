Jacobsthal's function: a quadratic bound

For a modulus n with k distinct prime factors, j(n) is the least interval length that guarantees an integer coprime to n. We compute j(n) and compare it to k².

Context

Proves h(k) ≤ C k²/(log log(3k))² for an absolute constant C>0 and every k≥1, answering Jacobsthal's quadratic-bound question: every interval of C k² consecutive integers contains an integer coprime to any prescribed positive integer with at most k distinct prime divisors. The bound is uniform over prime sets and interval positions and removes the classical logarithmic loss; intervals may begin at any signed integer.

Relevance

Removes the classical logarithmic loss from Jacobsthal's function bound, with applications to covering systems and prime gaps; the optimal order of growth remains undetermined.
