Exact crossing numbers of complete and complete bipartite graphs (conceptual, result 165)

Compares the exact crossing-number formulas for (K_n) and (K_{m,n}) with the crossing counts of simple straight-line drawings, illustrating the Harary--Hill and Zarankiewicz results.

Context

The crossing number of a graph is the minimum number of edge crossings in a plane drawing with continuous arcs. Result 165 resolves the Harary--Hill conjecture for complete graphs and Turán's brickyard problem in the Zarankiewicz formulation for complete bipartite graphs: every (K_n) has crossing number (\frac{1}{4}\lfloor n/2\rfloor\lfloor (n-1)/2\rfloor\lfloor (n-2)/2\rfloor\lfloor (n-3)/2\rfloor), and every (K_{m,n}) has crossing number (\lfloor m/2\rfloor\lfloor (m-1)/2\rfloor\lfloor n/2\rfloor\lfloor (n-1)/2\rfloor). It also proves that the classical drawings are optimal among all plane drawings with continuous edge arcs.

Relevance

These are the fundamental extremal constants for graph drawings. They give sharp lower bounds for wiring, VLSI layout, and network visualization, and they anchor topological graph theory: the gap between simple convex or bipartite straight-line drawings and the optimum measures how much careful routing can save. The result turns long-standing conjectures into exact formulas for two canonical graph families.

The simple layout is never better than the optimum and is strictly worse for sufficiently large graphs.
