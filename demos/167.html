Pinned distances and a power saving for planar unit distances (conceptual, result 167)

Generates a planar point set, counts distinct distances determined by each pin, and counts unit-distance pairs; compares with the weak pinned bound (n^{1-\varepsilon}) and the classical (n^{4/3}) unit-distance exponent.

Context

Proves the weak pinned Erdős distance conjecture: for every fixed \(\varepsilon>0\), all but \(o(n)\) points of any \(n\)-point planar set determine at least \(n^{1-\varepsilon}\) distinct nonzero distances. A complementary theorem bounds the number of unit-distance pairs by \(O(n^{4/3-\delta})\) for an absolute \(\delta>0). The demo samples finite point sets and measures both phenomena directly.

Relevance

Gives the first power improvement over the classical exponent (4/3) for planar unit distances and shows that large repeated distance fibers are rare. Together, these resolve the weak pinned distance problem and move the unit-distance problem beyond the long-standing (O(n^{4/3})) barrier, with constants uniform in (n).
