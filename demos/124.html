Polynomial-time scheduling on three identical machines (conceptual, result 124)

Unit-length jobs with arbitrary precedence constraints are scheduled on three identical machines. Adjust the job count, precedence density, and deadline; regenerate random instances and inspect the exact feasibility decision and an optimal makespan schedule.

Context

The problem is the three-processor unit-execution-time scheduling problem of Garey and Johnson: nonpreemptive unit-length jobs, arbitrary precedence constraints, three identical parallel machines. The result gives a deterministic polynomial-time algorithm that minimizes makespan. For an explicitly given precedence graph, it decides deadline feasibility exactly and constructs a feasible schedule. The conceptual model here uses a small instance and an exact backtracking search over available-job subsets at each time step, which is feasible for the displayed sizes and illustrates the exact decision procedure.

Relevance

This resolves a long-standing open scheduling problem by showing that minimizing makespan for unit jobs on three identical machines is polynomial, rather than NP-hard. It has direct consequences for deterministic scheduling, deadline feasibility checking, and the complexity boundary between polynomial and NP-hard parallel scheduling, since the same problem becomes NP-hard for four or more machines.
