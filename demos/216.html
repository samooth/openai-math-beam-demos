Critical logarithmic corrections and BKT scaling for the planar XY model (conceptual, result 216)

The planar cosine XY model undergoes a Berezinskii–Kosterlitz–Thouless transition: at criticality correlations decay as (C_{\beta_c}(r)\sim A r^{-1/4}(\log r)^{1/8}), while the correlation length diverges with the essential singularity (\sqrt{\beta_c-\beta}\log\xi(\beta)\to B). Vortex unbinding separates a rough low-temperature phase from exponential decay above (T_c).

Context

For the square-lattice nearest-neighbor cosine XY model, result 216 proves the critical axis correlation \(C_{\beta_c}(r)\sim A r^{-1/4}(\log r)^{1/8}\) and the BKT essential singularity \(\sqrt{\beta_c-\beta}\log \xi(\beta)\to B\), with \(A,B>0) after the free-box thermodynamic limit. For finite square-symmetric interactions containing nearest neighbors, discrete Gaussian heights converge to Gaussian fields throughout the rough phase, including its threshold, along geometric torus sizes. Critical center-magnetization and spin-field conclusions retain their stated height, renormalization, and field-input assumptions.

Relevance

Quantifies the universal critical decay and the correlation-length divergence at the BKT transition, supporting rough-phase Gaussian free-field limits and universality of planar spin and height fields. The demo interpolates the predicted decay laws across the transition so the logarithmic correction and essential singularity can be seen directly.
