Deterministic construction of strong thin spanning trees (conceptual, result 174)

Explores the strong thin-tree property: for a $k$-edge-connected multigraph, a spanning tree whose edges cross every cut at most a $C/k$ fraction of the original cut.

Context

The strong thin-tree conjecture asks for a spanning tree that crosses every cut sparsely relative to the original graph. Result 174 gives one absolute constant $C>0$ such that every finite loopless $k$-edge-connected multigraph with at least two vertices has a spanning tree crossing each nontrivial cut at most $(C/k)$ times the original cut size; parallel edges remain distinct. Moreover the tree can be found by a deterministic polynomial-time algorithm, even when parallel-edge multiplicities are encoded in binary. No numerical value of $C$ is asserted by the theorem; the demo uses a heuristic threshold to illustrate the property.

Relevance

Thin trees are a structural tool for graph decomposition, approximation, and embedding problems: a spanning tree that rarely crosses cuts approximates the connectivity structure of the original graph without paying the full cut size. Turning the conjecture into a deterministic polynomial-time construction makes the tool algorithmic rather than existential, with applications to routing, network design, and cut-based optimization. The demo compares naive trees with a locally optimized tree and measures the worst cut ratio exactly over all subsets.
