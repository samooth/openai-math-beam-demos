The Euclidean Steinitz–Bergström bound (conceptual, result 097)

Any finite sequence in the Euclidean unit ball of (\mathbb R^d) admits signs keeping every partial sum within (C\sqrt d), independently of length. Zero-sum families can be reordered with the same bound on unsigned partial sums; the optimal order is (S_2(d)=\Theta(\sqrt d)).

Context

Proves that any finite sequence in the Euclidean unit ball of (\mathbb R^d) admits signs keeping every partial sum within (C\sqrt d), independently of length. Consequently every zero-sum family can be reordered with the same bound on unsigned partial sums. A matching lower bound gives the optimal order (S_2(d)=\Theta(\sqrt d)). The formalized result is existential and does not supply an online algorithm.

Relevance

The bound is dimension-optimal for Euclidean partial sums, improving the classical Steinitz bound of order (O(d)). It underlies vector balancing, discrepancy theory, and combinatorial optimization, and motivates algorithmic and high-dimensional variants where constructive bounds remain open.
