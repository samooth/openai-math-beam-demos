Continuum phase transitions for radial pair potentials (conceptual, result 228)

Stable distance-dependent pair interactions for three-dimensional classical particles with a first-order temperature singularity: a canonical free-energy derivative jump at one inverse temperature throughout an open density interval.

Context

Constructs admissible radial pair potentials in $\mathbb R^3$ whose canonical free energy exists for every positive inverse temperature and density. One potential has a divergent repulsive core with an integrable tail that is $o(r^{-3})$; another is bounded and continuous with tail $|\phi(r)|\le C r^{-3-1/32}$. At a common inverse temperature $\beta_c\in(1/2,3/2)$, and for every density in a nonempty open interval, the free energy has a strict finite jump between its one-sided derivatives with respect to inverse temperature. This realizes the type of transition sought in Simon's continuum problem, including over a density interval rather than at a single density.

Relevance

Gives rigorous examples of first-order phase transitions in continuum particle systems with realistic radial interactions, separating the roles of short-range repulsion and long-range tail decay. The density-interval statement shows the singularity is not an isolated artifact: it persists across an open set of densities, supporting the use of radial potentials as models for temperature-driven condensation and for comparing canonical and grand-canonical descriptions.
