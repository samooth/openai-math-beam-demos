Nonsofic groups and group-ring counterexamples (conceptual, result 197)

Explores Kaplansky direct-finiteness, Gottschalk surjunctivity, and the group-ring Determinant Conjecture: in finite settings injectivity implies surjectivity, while the result produces infinite nonsofic counterexamples.

Context

Kaplansky's direct-finiteness conjecture says that in a group algebra $K[G]$, $ab=1$ implies $ba=1$. Gottschalk's surjunctivity conjecture says every injective cellular automaton on $K^G$ is surjective. The Determinant Conjecture says an invertible matrix over $\mathbb Z[G]$ has Fuglede--Kadison determinant at least $1$. Result 197 constructs a finitely presented torsion-free nonsofic group whose group algebra over $\mathbb F_2$ is not directly finite, giving $a,b$ with $ab=1$ but $ba\neq 1$; the induced cellular automaton on $K^G$ is injective but not surjective; and an integral group-ring matrix invertible over $\mathbb Q[G]$ has determinant strictly between $0$ and $1$.

Relevance

Shows that direct finiteness, surjunctivity, and determinant bounds can fail for infinite nonsofic groups, even in torsion-free characteristic two. Finite and amenable settings retain the implications; the counterexamples isolate nonsoficity as the obstruction.
