The sharp constant in random triangle removal (conceptual, result 188)

Starting from $K_n$, repeatedly delete the three edges of a uniformly chosen remaining triangle. The demo simulates this process and estimates the terminal edge count, which is asymptotic to $n^{3/2}/(2\sqrt2)$.

Context

Start with the complete graph on $n$ vertices. Repeatedly choose uniformly at random a remaining triangle and delete its three edges, until the graph is triangle-free. The number of edges at termination, divided by $n^{3/2}$, converges in $L^2$ to $1/(2\sqrt2)$; hence it also converges in probability and its normalized expectation converges to the same constant. This proves the triangle case of the sharp terminal-leave conjecture of Joos and Kühn.

Relevance

Determines the sharp leading constant for the terminal leave of the random triangle-removal process, giving a precise average-case endpoint for greedy triangle deletion and a concrete instance of the Joos–Kühn conjecture.
