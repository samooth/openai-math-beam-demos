Fourier transforms below n log n (conceptual, result 130)

A deterministic exact discrete Fourier transform of length $n$ can be computed with $O(n(\log n)^{1-\delta})$ operations, with explicit $\delta=10^{-13}$, beating the FFT's $n\log n$ bound by a sub-polynomial power saving.

Context

Result 130 gives a deterministic algorithm for the exact discrete Fourier transform of length $n$. In the model of exact complex arithmetic, with unrestricted coefficients and a supplied primitive $n$-th root of unity, it uses $O(n(\log n)^{1-\delta})$ scalar operations for every $n$, with explicit $\delta=10^{-13}$; scalar preparation and logarithmic-word indexing are counted. This is below the standard FFT bound of $\Theta(n\log n)$ by the factor $(\log n)^{\delta}$, a sub-polynomial saving that shows the exact DFT complexity is not pinned at $n\log n$ in this model.

Relevance

The $n\log n$ FFT bound has been the standard for decades, so any provable improvement, even with a tiny explicit $\delta=10^{-13}$, changes the complexity picture for exact linear transforms. It has consequences for fast convolution, signal processing, polynomial multiplication, and the search for stronger power savings or matching lower bounds.
