Memory–sample lower bounds for noiseless Gaussian regression (conceptual, result 140)

A one-pass learner observes $y_i=\langle a_i,\theta\rangle$ with $a_i\sim \mathcal N(0,I_d)$ for an unknown unit vector $\theta\in\mathbb R^d$. Slide dimension, accuracy, memory size, and regime to see the resulting sample lower bound $\Omega(d\log(1/\varepsilon))$.

Context

In noiseless Gaussian regression, a learner observes inner products of an unknown unit vector in $\mathbb R^d$ with independent standard Gaussian vectors. For fixed $A>0$, a one-pass learner retaining at most $Ad^2$ bits between observations needs $\Omega_A(d\log(1/\varepsilon))$ observations to recover the signal to angular error at most $\varepsilon$ with probability at least $2/3$, uniformly for large $d$, when $0<\varepsilon\le 1/10$. Computation and randomized updates are unrestricted, but output uses only the terminal state, stopping index, and fresh randomness. For subquadratic memory $o(d^2)$, the constant can be universal and the bound can be required for every signal.

Relevance

This quantifies the cost of memory in continuous statistical estimation: even with quadratic-bit memory and unlimited computation, one-pass recovery of a direction requires on the order of $d\log(1/\varepsilon)$ samples. It bears on streaming and memory-constrained inference, posterior-replica information bounds, exact Gaussian observation localization, and the design of memory-efficient estimators.
