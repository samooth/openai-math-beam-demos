The Benjamini–Schramm nonuniqueness conjecture (conceptual, result 214)

Simulates Bernoulli bond percolation on a finite truncation of a regular tree, the Cayley graph of a free group. Edges are open with probability $p$; clusters reaching the bottom boundary approximate infinite clusters. The tree has exact thresholds $p_c=1/b$ and $p_u=1/\sqrt b$ for branching number $b$.

Relevance

Distinguishes nonamenable geometry from amenable graphs such as $\mathbb Z^d$, where there is at most one infinite cluster for every $p>p_c$. The interval $(p_c,p_u)$ is a genuine multiple-cluster phase, with consequences for random walks, harmonic measure, and percolation on groups. The interactive model uses a regular tree with branching number $b$; its thresholds are $p_c=1/b$ and $p_u=1/\sqrt b$. Varying $b$, depth, and $p$ shows how boundary clusters proliferate precisely in the nonunique phase, while finite depth rounds the sharp thresholds.
