Talagrand’s conjectures and graph decompositions at expectation thresholds (conceptual, result 175)

Compares integral and fractional expectation thresholds on the increasing family of subsets of size at least (k), and illustrates the associated (p)-small covers, discrete convexity, and graph decomposition into a bounded number of pieces.

Context

For a finite ground set (X) and a nonempty proper increasing family (\mathcal F\subseteq 2^X), Talagrand's expectation-threshold conjecture asks whether the fractional and integral expectation thresholds are comparable by an absolute constant. Result 175 proves (q_f(\mathcal F)\le 25\cdot 512^4q(\mathcal F)), with both thresholds using cover budget (1/2). It also resolves Talagrand's discrete-convexity conjecture with (k=2^{75}): if a family has Bernoulli-(p) measure at least (1-1/k), then the sets not contained in a union of (k) members form a (p)-small family with containment cover cost at most (1/2). An application proves the Ascoli--He--Park--Talagrand graph-decomposition conjecture: every graph's edges split into a universally bounded number of fixed pieces, each with containment threshold at most a universal constant times the original graph's integral expectation threshold; the pieces' embeddings need not agree on shared vertices.

Relevance

This turns fractional certificates into integral ones at only a universal loss, preserving the (1/2) covering budget. It separates the random-set threshold from the certificate threshold, explains why singleton and low-cardinality fractional covers can be rounded, and supplies the discrete-convexity step needed for graph decompositions. The demo uses the standard threshold family (\{H\subseteq X:|H|\ge k\}), where the integral cover consists of (k)-subsets and the fractional cover uses singleton weights (1/k), making the two thresholds explicit and comparable.
