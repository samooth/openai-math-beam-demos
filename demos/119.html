The Courtade–Kumar and Hellinger conjectures (conceptual, result 119)

For Boolean functions of independent uniform bits, a single coordinate retains the most mutual information after independent bit-flip noise; the Hellinger profile loss is also maximized by a signed coordinate for every output bias and noise correlation. Explore the Hellinger loss, the retained mutual information, and the bias refinement.

Context

The formalization proves sharp contraction of the information carried by a binary channel under independent symmetric noise on a uniform discrete cube. At each fixed initial information level, a noisy coordinate channel attains the maximum retained information. It proves the Courtade–Kumar inequality $I(f(X);Y)\le 1-h_2(\varepsilon)$ in bits at crossover probability $0\le\varepsilon\le 1/2$, where $h_2$ is binary entropy, with a coordinate and its complement attaining equality. It also proves the Hellinger conjecture: for every Boolean $f:\{-1,1\}^n\to\{-1,1\}$ with mean $m$ and every $\rho\in[-1,1]$, $\sqrt{1-m^2}-\mathbb E\sqrt{1-(T_\rho f)^2}\le 1-\sqrt{1-\rho^2}$, with equality for signed coordinates. The refined Boolean bound accounts for output bias, with strict improvement for nonconstant biased outputs at nonzero noise correlation, together with a selected mean-dependent entropy-production inequality.

Relevance

This settles the optimal Boolean function for information retention under bit-flip noise and gives the exact Hellinger-profile extremal. Removing the balancedness restriction handles arbitrary output bias and all noise correlations, strengthening the implication to the Courtade–Kumar information bound. The mean-dependent entropy-production inequality controls instantaneous information loss along the noise semigroup and underlies the dimension induction.
