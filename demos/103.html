Derandomization of logarithmic space: $\mathsf L=\mathsf{RL}=\mathsf{BPL}$ (conceptual, result 103)

A randomized polynomial-time logarithmic-space machine with one-sided or two-sided error is compiled into a deterministic logarithmic-space decider. Slide the input size, space bound, and error tolerance to watch the seed length, number of enumerated seeds, and resulting deterministic space and time.

Context

The classes are $\mathsf L$ (deterministic logarithmic space), $\mathsf{RL}$ (randomized logarithmic space with one-sided error), and $\mathsf{BPL}$ (bounded-error probabilistic logarithmic space). The result proves $\mathsf L=\mathsf{RL}=\mathsf{BPL}$: every randomized machine using $O(\log n)$ space and polynomial time can be simulated deterministically in $O(\log n)$ space with explicit polynomial running-time bounds. The effective compiler replaces the random tape by a pseudorandom generator with a short seed, then enumerates all seeds and takes the majority or threshold vote. This removes randomness from small-space computation without increasing the space order.

Relevance

This resolves the central derandomization question for bounded-error logarithmic-space computation. It implies that randomness does not add power to logarithmic space, with consequences for streaming algorithms, small-space decision procedures, space-bounded pseudorandomness, and the design of deterministic algorithms under tight memory limits. The explicit polynomial bounds make the conversion constructive rather than merely existential.
