Zariski’s multiplicity conjecture: counterexample (conceptual, result 059)

A reduced holomorphic hypersurface germ with an isolated critical point has a multiplicity: the degree of its tangent cone. Zariski conjectured that ambient homeomorphism of such germs forces equal multiplicity. The counterexamples keep the ambient topological type fixed while changing that tangent-cone degree.

Context

Disproves Zariski's multiplicity conjecture by constructing reduced holomorphic hypersurface germs that are ambiently homeomorphic but have different multiplicities. The examples include hypersurfaces in (\mathbb C^4) with isolated critical points and multiplicities four and five.

Relevance

Shows that multiplicity is not determined by the ambient topology of a hypersurface germ: two germs can be ambiently homeomorphic while their tangent cones have different degrees. This motivates stronger invariants, such as Lipschitz geometry or metric tangent cones, for analytic classification.

Reading the picture

Each panel shows the same topological link (same number of components) on a small sphere around the origin. The radial segments at the center represent the tangent cone: their count is the multiplicity. The ambient homeomorphism preserves the link but need not preserve that count.
