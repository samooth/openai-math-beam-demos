Primitive roots for every admissible integer base

For a base a and prime p (not dividing a), a is a primitive root mod p iff its multiplicative order is p−1. We count such primes p≤N and compare with Artin's predicted density.

Context

Proves the infinitude assertion in Artin's primitive root conjecture: for every integer a that is neither −1 nor a square, there are at least c_a x/(log x)^2 primes in (x,2x) with primitive root a, for some c_a>0 and every sufficiently large x.

Relevance

Establishes that every admissible base is a primitive root for infinitely many primes, with a quantitative lower bound; relevant to Artin's conjecture, finite fields, and pseudorandomness.
