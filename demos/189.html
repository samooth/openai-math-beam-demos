Exact cycle-clique Ramsey numbers (conceptual, result 189)

For integers (m\ge n\ge 3), every red/blue coloring of (K_{(m-1)(n-1)+1}) contains a red cycle (C_m) or a blue clique (K_n), with the single exception (R(C_3,K_3)=6). Tune (m,n) to see the threshold and the extremal coloring on ((m-1)(n-1)) vertices.

Context

Result 189 proves the Erdős-Faudree-Rousseau-Schelp conjecture: (R(C_m,K_n)=(m-1)(n-1)+1) for all (m\ge n\ge 3), except (R(C_3,K_3)=6) (the formula would give (5)). Thus ((m-1)(n-1)) vertices are not enough, while one more vertex forces a red (m)-cycle or a blue (n)-clique. The lower bound colors (n-1) disjoint parts of size (m-1) as red cliques and all edges between different parts blue, avoiding both forbidden structures.

Relevance

This settles the exact cycle-clique Ramsey threshold, identifies the extremal coloring structure, and provides a sharp boundary case for Ramsey theory. It also supports finite obstruction and certificate methods by making the forcing transition completely explicit.

Extremal coloring

Take (n-1) disjoint red cliques of size (m-1), and color every edge between different cliques blue. The resulting graph has ((m-1)(n-1)) vertices, contains no red (C_m), and contains no blue (K_n). Adding one vertex forces a red (m)-cycle or a blue (n)-clique.
