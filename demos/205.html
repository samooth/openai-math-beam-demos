Saxl's conjecture and universal tensor squares (conceptual, result 205)

Saxl's conjecture: the tensor square of the staircase representation of $S_n$ contains every irreducible complex representation of $S_n$. The broader universal statement holds for every $S_n$ with $n\notin\{2,4,9\}$.

Context

Result 205 proves Saxl's conjecture: if $\rho_m=(m,m-1,\dots,1)$ is the staircase partition of $n=m(m+1)/2$, then the tensor square $\rho_m\otimes\rho_m$ contains every irreducible representation of $S_n$, equivalently every Kronecker coefficient $g(\rho_m,\rho_m,\mu)$ is positive. More generally, for every $n\notin\{2,4,9\}$ there exists an irreducible representation of $S_n$ whose tensor square contains all irreducibles; the cases $n=2,4,9$ are genuine exceptions. The demo below uses the character table of $S_n$ to test, for a chosen irreducible $\lambda$, whether $\lambda\otimes\lambda$ contains every irreducible $\mu$.

Relevance

This settles a central problem in the representation theory of symmetric groups and Kronecker coefficients, with implications for tensor products, plethysm, and quantum information (maximally entangled states). It distinguishes the staircase family, which is universal, from exceptional small degrees, and provides explicit irreducibles witnessing universality for all non-exceptional $n$. The interactive view lets you change $n$, choose an irreducible, and see exactly which irreducibles occur in its tensor square.
