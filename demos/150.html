Weak mixing of irrational triangular billiards (conceptual, result 150)

A billiard ball bounces inside a Euclidean triangle. Vary the angles and an irrational perturbation; the trajectory and its Poincaré section on side (AB) show whether directions remain finite (rational angles) or become dense (irrational angles, weakly mixing).

Context

Proves that the billiard flow in every nondegenerate Euclidean triangle with at least one angle irrational relative to (\pi) is weakly mixing for normalized area times uniform direction. This strengthens ergodicity on the entire irrational-angle class, with no genericity or Diophantine restrictions.

Relevance

Places all irrational-angle triangles in the weakly mixing regime, linking polygonal billiards to spectral theory and quantum chaos; distinguishes rational-angle (integrable, finite-direction) triangles from the irrational case and removes earlier genericity or Diophantine hypotheses.

Conceptual model: a finite trajectory cannot prove weak mixing, but irrational angle ratios produce dense direction sets and increasingly uniform Poincaré distributions, while rational angles yield finitely many directions. The irrational perturbation is (\delta\sqrt2) degrees, making at least one angle an irrational multiple of (\pi) whenever (\delta\neq0).
