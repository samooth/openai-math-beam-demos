Bloch’s conjecture for complex surfaces (conceptual, result 040)

For a smooth connected projective complex surface (S) with (p_g(S)=0), the Albanese map (\mathrm{CH}_0(S)^0\to\mathrm{Alb}(S)(\mathbb C)) on integral degree-zero zero-cycles is an isomorphism. Tune (p_g) and (q) to see when Bloch's conjecture applies.

Context

Proves Bloch's conjecture: for every smooth connected projective complex surface (S) with (p_g(S)=0), the Albanese map (\mathrm{CH}_0(S)^0\to\mathrm{Alb}(S)(\mathbb C)) on integral degree-zero zero-cycles is an isomorphism. This combines the new (p_g=q=0) theorem with the classical theorem of Bloch, Kas, and Lieberman.

Relevance

Identifies the surfaces with (p_g=0) as those whose degree-zero zero-cycles are completely captured by the Albanese variety; the map is an isomorphism, so the Chow group of zero-cycles modulo rational equivalence has no extra transcendental part. This links the geometry of complex surfaces to their Albanese and supports motive and zero-cycle computations.
