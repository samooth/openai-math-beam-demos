Oka classification for minimal compact complex surfaces (conceptual, result 042)

For a connected minimal compact complex surface: if Kodaira dimension (\kappa=0), it is Oka; if class VII ((\kappa=-\infty,b_1=1)), it is Oka exactly for Hopf or Enoki surfaces. Every complex K3 surface is Oka, including nonprojective ones. Tune the family and invariants below to see the classification.

Context

Proves that every complex K3 surface is Oka, including nonprojective surfaces, resolving the K3 Oka conjecture: holomorphic maps from neighborhoods of compact convex sets in (\mathbb C^m) can be approximated by entire maps. More generally, every connected minimal compact complex surface of Kodaira dimension zero is Oka; a connected minimal compact complex surface of class VII is Oka exactly when it is a Hopf or Enoki surface. Enriques surfaces follow by the K3 double cover; complex tori, bielliptic, and Kodaira surfaces are Oka by established cases.

Relevance

Identifies which minimal compact complex surfaces are Oka: all of Kodaira dimension zero, and among class VII only Hopf and Enoki. This gives holomorphic flexibility (dense entire curves with prescribed first jet and strong dominability for projective K3/Enriques) and supports the Oka principle on complex surfaces.
