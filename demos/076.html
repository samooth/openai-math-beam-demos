Ultraflat Littlewood polynomials (conceptual, result 076)

A real Littlewood polynomial is (P_N(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\varepsilon_k z^k) with (\varepsilon_k\in\{-1,1\}). Ultraflatness means (|P_N(z)|=(1+o(1))\sqrt N) uniformly on the unit circle, including the real endpoints (z=\pm1). Explore sign patterns, flatness, and the resulting (unbounded) binary merit factors.

Context

Proves that for every sufficiently large \(N\) there exist real Littlewood polynomials of degree \(N-1\) with coefficients in \(\{-1,1\}\) whose modulus satisfies \(\max_{|z|=1}|P_N(z)|\le (1+\eta)\sqrt N\) for any fixed \(\eta>0\). Hence real Littlewood polynomials are ultraflat on the entire unit circle, including \(z=\pm1\). A single family can be chosen for all lengths with \(L^p\)-flatness for every fixed \(p>0). The associated binary merit factors are unbounded, disproving Turyn's bounded-merit-factor conjecture. The statements are existential and give no effective rate or signing algorithm.

Relevance

Ultraflat Littlewood polynomials settle the optimal uniform size of (\pm1)-coefficient polynomials on the unit circle and show that binary sequences can have arbitrarily large merit factors. This matters for aperiodic autocorrelation, radar/sonar pulse design, and communication sequences, where high merit factor means low sidelobe energy. It also closes a classical extremal problem (Littlewood's flatness question) and motivates constructive or algorithmic searches for near-optimal sign patterns.
