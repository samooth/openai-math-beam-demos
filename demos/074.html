Kakeya in three and four dimensions (conceptual, result 074)

A Kakeya set contains a unit segment in every direction. Result 074 proves the three-dimensional Kakeya maximal conjecture and the four-dimensional Hausdorff-dimension conjecture. Explore a family of (\delta)-tubes, their shading density, and the resulting dimension/maximal estimates.

Context

In three dimensions, for every \(\varepsilon>0) the radius-(\delta) tube maximal operator maps (L^3(\mathbb R^3)) to (L^3(S^2)) with norm at most (C_\varepsilon \delta^{-\varepsilon}). In four dimensions, every set containing a unit segment in every direction has Hausdorff dimension four. The three-dimensional maximal estimate is the analytic endpoint behind the dimension statement; the four-dimensional result is the Hausdorff case, with no measurability or stickiness hypothesis on the witnessing segments.

Relevance

These are the endpoint Kakeya results in dimensions three and four. They feed restriction theory, Nikodym maximal estimates, curved Kakeya problems, and geometric measure theory: full Hausdorff dimension in four dimensions and the sharp (L^3) maximal bound in three dimensions constrain how tubes can overlap and how sets containing lines in many directions can be small. The four-dimensional theorem removes the last missing Hausdorff case at this dimension, while the three-dimensional maximal theorem supplies the density-power estimate needed for analytic applications.
