Erdős short Egyptian fractions

Greedy Egyptian-fraction decomposition of a/b into distinct unit fractions; compare the number of terms to log log b.

Context

Every rational a/b with 1≤a<b is a sum of O(log log b) distinct positive unit fractions. The worst-case minimum number of terms has the same order, resolving Erdős’s conjecture on short Egyptian fractions. For every sufficiently large b, every such a/b admits an expansion with an absolute implied constant; this order is best possible when the numerator varies. The number of exact k-term expansions of 1 and the least integer ≥2 missing from all of them both grow doubly exponentially in k (double logarithms of order k).

Relevance

Settles the optimal order of Egyptian-fraction expansions and underlies counting and denominator-growth phenomena (doubly exponential in the number of terms).
