Counterexamples to Ryser’s covering conjecture (conceptual, result 162)

Compares the Ryser bound ( \tau(H)\le (r-1)\nu(H) ) with an intersecting (r)-partite (r)-uniform hypergraph whose covering number is ( \tau(H)=r ), and explores the truncated projective-plane equality case versus the counterexample case.

Context

Ryser’s covering conjecture asserts that every (r)-partite (r)-uniform hypergraph satisfies (\tau(H)\le (r-1)\nu(H)), where (\tau) is the covering number and (\nu) is the matching number. In the intersecting case (\nu=1), this predicts a cover of size at most (r-1). Result 162 disproves this: for every sufficiently large prime (s\equiv 2\pmod 3) and every sufficiently large odd (n), with (q=s^n), there is an intersecting ((q+1))-partite ((q+1))-uniform hypergraph with covering number (q+1). Thus at rank (r=q+1) the covering number is (r), one more than the conjectured bound.

Relevance

This settles the intersecting case of Ryser’s conjecture in the negative and produces infinitely many ranks (r=p^n+1) at which the covering inequality fails. It also refutes related covering formulations, including Gyárfás’s monochromatic tree-cover conjecture, by converting a hypergraph cover into a cover of a complete graph by connected monochromatic subgraphs. The construction shows that finite-plane equality examples can be modified by exceptional edges to raise the covering number beyond the classical bound.
