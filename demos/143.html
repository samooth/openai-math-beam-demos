Uniform limit-cycle bounds in Hilbert's sixteenth problem (conceptual, result 143)

Phase portrait of the Liénard system $x'=y-F(x)$, $y'=-x$ for a quintic $F$. Drag the coefficients to see equilibria, separatrices and closed orbits; the uniform bound for degree at most five is at most two limit cycles.

Context

Hilbert's sixteenth problem asks for a uniform bound $H(n)$ on the number of isolated periodic orbits (limit cycles) of a real planar polynomial vector field of degree $n$. This result resolves the uniform boundedness assertion: for every degree $n$ there is a finite constant depending only on $n$. For classical quintic Liénard systems $x'=y-F(x)$, $y'=-x$ with $\deg F\le 5$, the exact maximum is two limit cycles, with no sign, parity, hyperbolicity or amplitude restriction on $F$. The portrait below integrates the flow for a chosen quintic $F$.

Relevance

Settles the boundedness half of Hilbert's sixteenth problem and gives a sharp small-degree benchmark (two cycles for quintic Liénard systems), guiding bifurcation analysis, normal-form studies and the search for extremal polynomial vector fields.
