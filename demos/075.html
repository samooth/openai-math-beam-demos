L log L Fourier convergence (conceptual, result 075)

For $f\in L\log L(\mathbb T)$, the symmetric partial sums $S_N f(x)=\sum_{k=-N}^N \widehat f(k)e^{ikx}$ converge to $f(x)$ for almost every $x$. Adjust the function and $N$ to watch the partial sums track $f$ pointwise, including at discontinuities where Gibbs overshoot persists but the value at the point still converges.

Context

Proves that for every measurable complex-valued function on $\mathbb T=\mathbb R/(2\pi\mathbb Z)$ with $\int_{\mathbb T}|f|\log(2+|f|)<\infty$, the full symmetric Fourier partial sums converge almost everywhere: outside one measurable null set, $\sum_{k=-N}^{N}\widehat f(k)e^{ikx}\to f(x)$ as $N\to\infty$. This resolves the classical sufficiency conjecture at the $L\log L$ integrability scale.

Relevance

Shows that the $L\log L$ Orlicz condition is exactly the natural borderline for almost-everywhere convergence of ordinary symmetric partial sums: it is weaker than $L^p$ for $p>1$ yet strong enough to rule out the $L^1$ counterexamples. It underpins harmonic analysis on the circle, maximal-function and singular-integral methods, and any application needing pointwise reconstruction from Fourier coefficients at the critical integrability scale.
