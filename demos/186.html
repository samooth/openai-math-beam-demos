Uniform influence and sharp thresholds for graph and hypergraph properties (conceptual, result 186)

For a nontrivial increasing graph property invariant under vertex permutations, the edge probability interval from probability $\varepsilon$ to $1-\varepsilon$ has width $O((\log n)^{-2})$. The demo estimates the transition for the extremal clique-containment property $K_k\subseteq G(n,p)$ and compares it with the universal bound and a Poisson approximation.

Context

For every $n\ge2$, every nontrivial increasing vertex-symmetric family $\mathcal P$ of graphs on $n$ vertices, and $0<\varepsilon<1/2$, the transition width satisfies $p_{1-\varepsilon}(\mathcal P)-p_\varepsilon(\mathcal P)\le 2^{19}\log(1/(2\varepsilon))/(\log n)^2$. The companion variance bound $\operatorname{Var}_p(f)\le 2^{17} I_p(f)/(\log n)^2$ holds for arbitrary (even nonmonotone) vertex-symmetric Boolean functions. For $r$-uniform hypergraphs the width bound is $O_r((\log n)^{-r/(r-1)})$. The squared logarithm is optimal: containing a clique of size proportional to $\log n$ has transition width $\Theta((\log n)^{-2})$.

Relevance

This resolves the Friedgut–Kalai sharp-threshold conjecture, removing the iterated-logarithm loss of earlier influence bounds and giving uniform control across all biases $p$. It applies to arbitrary increasing graph properties and to nonmonotone hypergraph properties, with implications for random graph thresholds, extremal examples, and the influence method. The demo illustrates the transition window on the clique property, which attains the optimal order, alongside the universal bound.
