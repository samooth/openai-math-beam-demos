Zero entropy does not guarantee a smooth positive-volume model (conceptual, result 152)

A standard nonatomic probability space carries an ergodic invertible transformation with zero Kolmogorov-Sinai entropy, yet it is not measurably conjugate to any $C^\infty$ diffeomorphism preserving a strictly positive smooth probability density on a compact finite-dimensional manifold. One example excludes every finite dimension.

Context

Result 152 gives a negative answer to the smooth positive-volume realization problem already in zero entropy. A smooth model would force, at dyadic times $n=2^k$, a partition of the manifold into at most $\exp(C B_k n)$ cells with $B_k$ summable, and a switch with output measure bounded by twice the original volume. The constructed symbolic system has high window entropy in short label lists and orthogonality constraints that cannot be reconciled with that smooth cost. Hence zero Kolmogorov-Sinai entropy does not suffice for realization by a smooth volume-preserving diffeomorphism.

Relevance

This separates finite entropy from smooth positive-volume realizability and shows that regularity and dimension impose constraints beyond metric entropy. It guides the smooth realization problem: one needs invariants beyond Kolmogorov-Sinai entropy, such as slow entropy or derivative-growth profiles, to decide whether an abstract system admits a smooth volume model.
